# S6.0: playbook data (scripts); S7.0: sector playbook (deepest tier, one sector per helper); S7R.n: playbook recheck (deep tier)

A playbook is written only for the sectors S5 chose (`process/s5_sector_map.md`, item 11). It studies earlier Indian tailwind episodes of the same kind as the sector's current one: who captured the tailwind, who didn't and why, and what signalled its end. It ends in sector notes for `process/investing_rules.md` (the orchestrator may add sector notes; a change to a general rule goes to the user for approval, `COMMON_AI.md`, "Rules file"). Playbooks live in `runs/playbooks/<sector id>/` and are reused across runs while current (12 months, and none of the sector's markers broken since).

## The playbook base data (`data/playbook/`)
Built 2026-11-03 and kept for the playbooks; scripts find it through `tools/scripts/config.py` (`playbook_dir()`, or the `PLAYBOOK_DATA` environment variable).
- `universe.csv` and `universe/` (every listed company in Nov 2026 plus 136 delisted candidates), `outcomes.csv` (per company and quarter-end start since Mar 2006: price and market-cap multiples over 2, 3, 5, 7 and 10 years, the peak inside each window, and what was visible at the start), `sector_test.md` (industry concentration of winners, `mb_sector_test.py`).
- Features: `features.csv` (sample), `market_features.csv` and `market_features_full.csv` (every company at each quarter-end), `features_panel.csv`, `features_exit.csv` and `exit_rows.csv` (fallen winners against winners that held up), `features_winners_ar.csv`; each with its `_coverage.md`.
- `sample.csv`, `sample_candidates.csv` (winners and fallen winners per horizon), `business_types.csv`, `business_types_list.md`, `business_types/` (economic business types of about 600 companies, as of each window start), `peer_panel.csv`, `patterns.csv`, `patterns_meta.json`, `market_winners_disc.csv`, `market_winners_test.csv` (pattern results so far), `experts.md` (34 findings from named practitioners, hypotheses H1-H20), annual-report state (`ar_plan.csv`, `ar_status.csv`, `ar_links.csv`, `ar_missing.csv`; `tools/ar_statements.md`), and `status.md` (that run's history, frozen).
- A yearly refresh writes new files in place; set the old ones aside in `archive/playbook_<date>/` first.

## Business type (when a sector spans different economics)
Classify each company by what it actually does and what drives its results, never by a provider's label. Keep the type list short and economic, e.g. branded consumer, specialty manufacturer (engineered/B2B products), contract manufacturer/CDMO, capital goods and order-book businesses, EPC/infrastructure contractor, commodity producer or processor, lender, insurer/AMC/broker/platform, IT and knowledge services, healthcare services, pharma (generic/specialty/API), retail and distribution, real-estate developer, regulated utility, conglomerate/holding company; add a type only when companies don't fit (in `data/playbook/business_types/new_types*.md`). The base data's `business_types.csv` already types about 600 companies as of each window start; reuse it, and type others from `scr/overview.json`'s about text first, a window-start annual report only when that leaves the type unclear (`mb_tag_batch.py` and `mb_sample.py --finalize` build and check tagging batches if a playbook needs many).

## Pattern tests (short form; every pattern a playbook proposes)
1. **Point in time:** measured only from data published before the episode's start (results from their filing date, or quarter end + 45 days, + 60 for Q4; prices and P/E from the chart series on that date; `mb_features.py`'s header). A pattern resting on a figure restated later is not used.
2. **Lift:** the pattern's share among companies that captured the tailwind, divided by its share among the sector's companies that didn't, on the same start date. Proposed only with a lift of 1.5 or more and a frequency of 50% or more among the captured; raw values are never compared across dates.
3. **Reason required:** a mechanism (earnings growth plus re-rating, reinvestment at high returns, operating leverage, supply discipline, balance-sheet repair) backed by the episode studies or `experts.md`. A strong number with no reason is reported as "possibly chance" and not proposed.
4. **Out of sample:** found on episodes starting before 2016, held unchanged on later ones (or on another sector's episodes of the same tailwind kind); thresholds checked at nearby values. The number of patterns tried is reported with the results. Infrequent patterns (turnarounds, spin-offs, windfalls) stay low-weight situational notes.

## S7.0: sector playbook (helper, deepest tier, one sector)
Brief: the sector's entry in `sectors.json` and `sector_map.md`, `runs/playbooks/<sector>/data.md` and `episodes.csv`, and the run folder. Working directory: the project root. Write only in `runs/playbooks/<sector>/`.
1. **Episodes:** for each episode in `data.md`, its tailwind kind and whether it matches today's (same kind, similar driver); use only matching ones, or say why a different kind still teaches something.
2. **Episode studies** (about 3-6 companies per playbook: those that captured, those that lagged or fell back, picked across episodes and sizes). For each:
   - **No hindsight:** for each stage, read only documents dated before it (annual reports from the screener list back to about 2013, results, transcripts and presentations from BSE/NSE; older years only from what was filed then; `process/data_rules.md`, `tools/tools.md`). Downloads into a temporary folder outside the project or `data/companies/<SYM>/<FYyyQn>/` per `process/data_rules.md`.
   - **The actual business:** what it does, how it earns, what drives revenue and margins (volumes, prices, order book, capacity, mix, regulation), and how that changed across the episode.
   - **Stages:** overlooked, earnings turn, re-rating, compounding, de-rating; for each, the dates, the trigger, how long it lasted, and the price, P/E and EPS change.
   - **What was visible at the time:** at each stage start, the signals an investor could have seen in the filings and figures then; for a laggard or fallen winner, why the tailwind didn't reach its profits (supply response, no pricing power, balance sheet, governance) and the signs before the fall.
3. **Experts:** search the web for India-specific evidence on this sector's cycles from proven, named practitioners and research houses (e.g. Motilal Oswal's Wealth Creation Studies, Marcellus, named fund managers' published letters); prefer the original report to news. For each finding: the claim, the evidence behind it (sample, period, method), the source (person or firm, title, date, link), and whether it holds in India. Add findings to `data/playbook/experts.md` under the sector, keeping hypothesis numbers stable.
4. **Synthesis:** who captured and why; what the capture test should look for now; how long such tailwinds lasted and what ended them (the end signals, dated, and whether they were visible in time); the horizon and stage implications; the measurable patterns, each through the pattern tests above (`playbook_data.py` and `mb_features.py` give the numbers; a pattern found in the studied companies is scored on the episode companies that weren't studied).
5. **Write** `runs/playbooks/<sector>/playbook.md` (episodes, studies, synthesis, every figure with its sample size and source, the caveats in `process/README.md`) and `notes_proposed.md`: sector notes worded as sub-points under the general rule they adjust (`process/investing_rules.md` structure, tagged **[Learned: SYM, document]** or **[Expert: …]**), situational notes marked low weight, any proposed general-rule change with old and new wording, and patterns not adopted with the reason. Add suggested markers for the sector entry if the episodes show better end signals.

Reply with a 5-line summary: episodes used, who captured and why, end signals, notes proposed, patterns rejected.

## S7R.n: playbook recheck (deep tier, with `process/recheck.md`)
Work file: `runs/playbooks/<sector>/playbook.md` with `notes_proposed.md`; output `playbook_rc<N>.md` and `notes_proposed_rc<N>.md`. Check dates, figures and the no-hindsight rule against the cited documents; that each proposed note passed every pattern test and has a mechanism; over-fitting (thresholds, few episodes); and whether a future analyst could apply each note at the time. A second recheck only if the first changed a proposed note.

## For the orchestrator
After the last recheck: add the agreed sector notes to `process/investing_rules.md` (and the sector to its "Sectors with notes" table), tell the user what changed (old and new wording, reason, evidence), send general-rule proposals to the user for approval, update the sector's markers in `sectors.json` if the playbook suggested better ones (log the change with `sector_calls.py log`), and only then let that sector's companies reach S13.
