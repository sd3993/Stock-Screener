# S2: sample (script, then an Opus medium review)

From `outcomes.csv` (S1), pick about 400 companies, each with its reason, into `studies/multibaggers/sample.csv` (sym, group, horizon, window start, start market cap, sector, reason, matched_to).

**Groups, per horizon** (only companies with a start market cap of ₹50 cr or more):
- **Winners:** short = price 2x+ in 2 years; medium = 3x+ in 5 years (about 25% CAGR, the hurdle), with 5x+ as the strong group; long = 10x+ in 7-10 years. The per-share price multiple must roughly match the market-cap multiple; where it doesn't, the gap is dilution (or buybacks) and is noted.
- **Controls:** about one per winner: a company that looked similar at the same window start (same market-cap band and business type, similar starting growth, P/E and P/E against its own median) and did not reach the winner threshold. Shortlist ~5 candidates per winner by the numbers and screener's industry, tag their business type (below) and pick one of the winner's type. Controls are what make lift measurable (`README.md`, "What a pattern must pass").
- **Fallen winners:** reached the winner threshold inside the window, then gave back more than 50% from the peak (`pxpeak_*` against the end multiple). They test the exit rules.
- **Delisted and merged** names wherever their history covers the window.

**Business type (helper, Opus medium, batches of ~25):** classify each company by what it actually does and what drives its results, the same way P5 does (`process/p5_sector_map.md` step 1): screener's "about" text (`scr/overview.json`), the segment split and business description in an annual report or presentation (from the exchange links in `scr/documents.json`), as of the window start where the business has changed since. Provider sector labels are only a starting hint. Write `studies/multibaggers/business_types.csv` (sym, as_of, business_type, sub_type, revenue drivers, cyclicality: low/medium/high, capital intensity: low/medium/high, pricing power: low/medium/high, customers: B2C/B2B/government, evidence). Keep the type list short and economic, e.g. branded consumer, specialty manufacturer (engineered/B2B products), contract manufacturer/CDMO, capital goods and order-book businesses, EPC/infrastructure contractor, commodity producer or processor, lender, insurer/AMC/broker/platform, IT and knowledge services, healthcare services, pharma (generic/specialty/API), retail and distribution, real-estate developer, regulated utility, conglomerate/holding company; add a type only when companies don't fit, and record the list with its definitions at the top of the file. A company whose mix changed (e.g. commodity to specialty) is typed as of each window start. Tag winners, controls, fallen winners and control shortlists here; S4 tags more companies only as needed. Before tagging, the orchestrator runs `scr_company.py <SYM> --only overview,documents` for each company to tag.

**Spread:** window starts across 2006-2023 so the 2008 crash, the 2011-13 slump, the 2018-19 mid-cap bear market, the 2020 crash and the 2023-24 boom all appear, with enough in 2006-2015 (discovery set) and 2016-2023 (test set) for each horizon. A company counts once per horizon (its first qualifying window) so long runs don't crowd the sample.

**Script:** the orchestrator writes the selection as a short script in the session scratchpad (or extends an existing one in `tools/scripts/` if a re-run will need it again), seeded so the picks are reproducible, and prints counts per group, horizon, period and size band.

**Review (helper, Opus medium):** reads `sample.csv` and the counts and checks: groups and periods balanced; controls genuinely comparable at the start (not already obviously different); no data artefacts posing as winners (price series breaks, splits or bonuses not adjusted, near-zero starting market caps, shell companies). It returns the rows to drop or replace with reasons; the orchestrator applies them and records the final counts in `status.md`.
