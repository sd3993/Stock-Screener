# S2: sample (script; business types by Opus medium helpers)

From `outcomes.csv` (S1), pick about 400 companies, each with its reason, into `runs/historical/<date>/sample.csv` (sym, group, horizon, window start, start market cap, sector, reason, matched_to).

**Groups, per horizon** (only companies with a start market cap of ₹50 cr or more):
- **Winners:** short = price 2x+ in 2 years; medium = 3x+ in 5 years (about 25% CAGR, the hurdle), with 5x+ as the strong group; long = 10x+ in 7-10 years. The per-share price multiple must roughly match the market-cap multiple; where it doesn't, the gap is dilution (or buybacks) and is noted.
- **Controls:** about one per winner: a company that looked similar at the same window start (same market-cap band and business type, similar starting growth, P/E and P/E against its own median) and did not reach the winner threshold. Shortlist ~5 candidates per winner by the numbers and screener's industry, tag their business type (below) and pick one of the winner's type. Controls are what make lift measurable (`README.md`, "What a pattern must pass").
- **Fallen winners:** reached the winner threshold inside the window, then gave back more than 50% from the peak (`pxpeak_*` against the end multiple). They test the exit rules.
- **Delisted and merged** names wherever their history covers the window.

**Business type (helper, Opus medium, batches of ~25):** classify each company by what it actually does and what drives its results, the same way P5 does (`process/future/p5_sector_map.md` step 1): screener's "about" text and industry label (`scr/overview.json`: `about` and `sectors`, screener's copy of NSE's classification) first; the segment split and business description in an annual report or presentation (from the exchange links in `scr/documents.json`) only when the about text leaves the type unclear, the company has several segments, or the business may have changed since the window start (user's choice, 3 Nov 2026, to save usage). The industry label is a coarse hint, never the answer: it is today's classification (wrong after a demerger or a change of business) and catch-all bins such as "Industrial Products" say little, so the about text or the window-start annual report decides; each batch file records whether it helped or misled (column `peers`, kept under that name: helped/misled/neutral). Screener's peers table isn't read: it is just the same industry's companies nearest in market cap, and it misled on about 15% of rows in the first six batches (3 Nov 2026). Provider sector labels are only a starting hint. Write `runs/historical/<date>/business_types.csv` (sym, as_of, business_type, sub_type, revenue drivers, cyclicality: low/medium/high, capital intensity: low/medium/high, pricing power: low/medium/high, customers: B2C/B2B/government, evidence). Keep the type list short and economic, e.g. branded consumer, specialty manufacturer (engineered/B2B products), contract manufacturer/CDMO, capital goods and order-book businesses, EPC/infrastructure contractor, commodity producer or processor, lender, insurer/AMC/broker/platform, IT and knowledge services, healthcare services, pharma (generic/specialty/API), retail and distribution, real-estate developer, regulated utility, conglomerate/holding company; add a type only when companies don't fit (in `business_types/new_types*.md`); the list with its definitions goes to `business_types_list.md` beside the CSV (see Script). A company whose mix changed (e.g. commodity to specialty) is typed as of each window start. Tag winners, controls, fallen winners and control candidates here; S4 tags more companies only as needed.

**Order (fewer tags, nothing lost to a usage cut-off):**
- Fetch once for S2 and S3: one background run, winners and fallen winners first, then candidates in distance order (`scr_company.py --syms-file <list> --only statements,quarterly,shareholding,peers,overview,documents,announcements,nse --skip-saved`), so S3 needs no second pass. Tagging helpers start on the first batches of 25 while it runs.
- Tag in rounds: winners, fallen winners and each winner's nearest candidate first; then, only for winners still without a same-type control, their next candidate; and so on. The pick rule (nearest candidate of the winner's type) is unchanged; candidates past the first match are never tagged.
- Each helper writes its own batch file (`business_types/batch_<n>.csv`); the orchestrator merges them into `business_types.csv`. A helper cut off mid-batch loses only that batch, which is re-run.

The selection itself is `tools/scripts/mb_sample.py` (see Script).

**Spread:** window starts across 2006-2023 so the 2008 crash, the 2011-13 slump, the 2018-19 mid-cap bear market, the 2020 crash and the 2023-24 boom all appear, with enough in 2006-2015 (discovery set) and 2016-2023 (test set) for each horizon. A company counts once per horizon (its first qualifying window) so long runs don't crowd the sample.

**Script:** `python -I tools/scripts/mb_sample.py [--seed 1]` writes `sample_candidates.csv` (winners, fallen winners, ~5 control candidates per winner, each with its reason), seeded so the picks are reproducible, and prints counts per group, horizon, period and size band. `--repair <old sample_candidates.csv>` (after outcomes.csv changes, e.g. a fix) keeps each old winner and fallen winner whose first qualifying window and size band are unchanged, replaces the rest from the same stratum (group, horizon, period, size band; seeded; never a company already picked), recomputes every winner's candidates, and writes `sample_repair.md` (kept, replaced and why, candidate lists changed) and `fetch_order.txt` (companies without `scr/overview.json`: new picks first, then candidates by rank); tags stay valid, as they are per (sym, as_of). `python -I tools/scripts/mb_sample.py --finalize` (after each tagging round; `--companies <dir>` only for tests; `--dir DIR` reads and writes DIR instead of the run folder, for tests) does the checks below and writes `business_types.csv` (all `batch_<n>.csv` merged, with a `batch` column), `business_types_list.md` (the type list with definitions, from this file and `new_types*.md`), `sample.csv` (passing winners with their control, the controls, passing fallen winners, each with its type and any warnings), `needs_next.csv` (the next round's tagging input: untagged winners and fallen winners, and each winner's next untagged candidate; a flagged or artefact winner asks for nothing) and `sample_checks.md` (counts, peers table, balance table, flagged rows, artefacts, warnings, malformed tag values, types not in the list, conflicting tags, and each winner's control status). An artefact needs evidence, since screener's prices are adjusted for splits and bonuses: a weekly price step of 1.5x or more that the market-cap-to-sales ratio contradicts beyond its one-decimal rounding, or that sits on a split/bonus ex-date (saved NSE corporate actions), a one-week spike that sets a fallen winner's peak, a step into the window end, a gap over 90 days hiding such a step, a stale start price, or a start market cap under ₹100 cr with a median weekly traded value under ₹5 lakh or 4+ of the 13 weeks before the start without volume. Steps it can't verify and a price multiple over twice the market-cap multiple are only warnings (the market cap from screener's ratios can be off). A candidate that is flagged or fails a check is skipped as a control.

**Checks (script, no helper):** `mb_sample.py` merges the batch files, picks each winner's control (nearest candidate of the same type), counts the peers flags, and checks: groups, periods and size bands balanced; no data artefacts posing as winners (a price jump with no matching market-cap change, i.e. an unadjusted split or bonus; a start market cap near the ₹50 cr floor with thin trading; a series break); controls within the matching limits. Shell companies or ones with no real operations are flagged by the tagging helpers, who read the about text anyway. The orchestrator drops or replaces flagged rows, re-runs the checks and records the final counts in `status.md`.
