# S2: sample (script, then an Opus medium review)

From `outcomes.csv` (S1), pick about 400 companies, each with its reason, into `runs/historical/<date>/sample.csv` (sym, group, horizon, window start, start market cap, sector, reason, matched_to).

**Groups, per horizon** (only companies with a start market cap of ₹50 cr or more):
- **Winners:** short = price 2x+ in 2 years; medium = 3x+ in 5 years (about 25% CAGR, the hurdle), with 5x+ as the strong group; long = 10x+ in 7-10 years. The per-share price multiple must roughly match the market-cap multiple; where it doesn't, the gap is dilution (or buybacks) and is noted.
- **Controls:** about one per winner: a company that looked similar at the same window start (same market-cap band and business type, similar starting growth, P/E and P/E against its own median) and did not reach the winner threshold. Shortlist ~5 candidates per winner by the numbers and screener's industry, tag their business type (below) and pick one of the winner's type. Controls are what make lift measurable (`README.md`, "What a pattern must pass").
- **Fallen winners:** reached the winner threshold inside the window, then gave back more than 50% from the peak (`pxpeak_*` against the end multiple). They test the exit rules.
- **Delisted and merged** names wherever their history covers the window.

**Business type (helper, Opus medium, batches of ~25):** classify each company by what it actually does and what drives its results, the same way P5 does (`process/future/p5_sector_map.md` step 1): screener's "about" text (`scr/overview.json`), the segment split and business description in an annual report or presentation (from the exchange links in `scr/documents.json`), as of the window start where the business has changed since. Provider sector labels are only a starting hint. Write `runs/historical/<date>/business_types.csv` (sym, as_of, business_type, sub_type, revenue drivers, cyclicality: low/medium/high, capital intensity: low/medium/high, pricing power: low/medium/high, customers: B2C/B2B/government, evidence). Keep the type list short and economic, e.g. branded consumer, specialty manufacturer (engineered/B2B products), contract manufacturer/CDMO, capital goods and order-book businesses, EPC/infrastructure contractor, commodity producer or processor, lender, insurer/AMC/broker/platform, IT and knowledge services, healthcare services, pharma (generic/specialty/API), retail and distribution, real-estate developer, regulated utility, conglomerate/holding company; add a type only when companies don't fit, and record the list with its definitions at the top of the file. A company whose mix changed (e.g. commodity to specialty) is typed as of each window start. Tag winners, controls, fallen winners and control shortlists here; S4 tags more companies only as needed. Before tagging, the orchestrator fetches the companies to tag in one background run (`scr_company.py --syms-file <list> --only overview,documents --skip-saved`) and starts the tagging helpers on the first batches of 25 while the rest is fetching. The selection itself is `tools/scripts/mb_sample.py` (see Script).

**Spread:** window starts across 2006-2023 so the 2008 crash, the 2011-13 slump, the 2018-19 mid-cap bear market, the 2020 crash and the 2023-24 boom all appear, with enough in 2006-2015 (discovery set) and 2016-2023 (test set) for each horizon. A company counts once per horizon (its first qualifying window) so long runs don't crowd the sample.

**Script:** `python -I tools/scripts/mb_sample.py [--seed 1]` writes `sample_candidates.csv` (winners, fallen winners, ~5 control candidates per winner, each with its reason), seeded so the picks are reproducible, and prints counts per group, horizon, period and size band. Once types are tagged, each winner's control is the nearest candidate of the same business type.

**Review (helper, Opus medium):** reads `sample.csv` and the counts and checks: groups and periods balanced; controls genuinely comparable at the start (not already obviously different); no data artefacts posing as winners (price series breaks, splits or bonuses not adjusted, near-zero starting market caps, shell companies). It returns the rows to drop or replace with reasons; the orchestrator applies them and records the final counts in `status.md`.
