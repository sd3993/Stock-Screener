You analyse one company in depth and write one analysis file (`main`); two rechecks then correct it (`/workflow/recheck.md`). Never read an earlier analysis of this company unless your brief says this is a re-research, and then only after your own analysis is finished, to write the "what changed" note. Do not edit `/principles/investing_rules.md`, the sheet or any file outside your output. Working directory: the project root (PowerShell). Your brief gives the company, the run folder (`runs/<FYyyQn>/`), the mode, whether the company is a holding, whether this is a re-research, and in recheck mode the S9 result that sent it (`<run>/marker_check.csv` rows).

## The analysis
Apply `/principles/investing_rules.md` throughout, weighing what the company passes against what it fails. Your results must not depend on today's share price: give EPS paths, exit P/Es and judgements only; the sheet computes P/E, PEG, multiples, CAGR and the buy-below price from live prices. The one exception is the new-candidate test of a broken thesis (step 13).
1. **Figures:** resolve every `facts_check.json` mismatch against the filing (rule out definition gaps first); list each genuine source-vs-filing mismatch (screener or another provider) as a data flag. Fetch anything missing yourself (per `/principles/data_rules.md`), say what you fetched, and add a line to `/workflow/fetch_learnings.md` (re-read it just before writing; no duplicates) so S10 fetches it next time.
2. **Normalised trailing EPS:** strip one-offs (the earnings evidence rule).
3. **Non-negotiables** (the non-negotiables): Pass / Bent / Fail for each of the four, with the evidence and its source; name any that fail.
4. **Every negative** (the trajectory rule): the cause with its source, temporary or structural, management's record on it, whether it is improving now. Keep a guidance-vs-delivery scorecard for the last 4-8 quarters.
5. **Sector case:** where the company's sector has a map entry, is the company capturing the tailwind (its own orders, capacity, share, margins against the sector's profit-capture test), or riding the label without it? A company with no sector entry is judged on its own drivers the same way. For an emerging company or a participant (`/principles/sector_rules.md`): what in its own filings shows the emerging business (orders, approvals, capex, a segment line), and what is still awaited; for a participant, which part the thesis rests on (established, emerging or both) and how much of the base case each supplies.
6. **Moat** per the moats rule: its kinds, the evidence for each, the threats, and the verdict (none / narrow / wide); say which inputs below it supports.
7. **Visibility and stage** per the visibility rule and the holding period rule: the visible-until date with the evidence that sets it; `years`, the projection length (whole fiscal years from the results used to the visible-until year, at least 2 and at most 7); and the discovery stage (the sector's from `sectors.json`, or the company's own with a reason: e.g. an under-owned name in a crowded sector).
8. **EPS paths:** base EPS at year 1; base and bear EPS at year `years`. Build them from guidance (haircut by the scorecard), order book, capacity coming online and margins; say how.
9. **Exit P/E** at that year, set exactly as the valuation and return math rule says, including both sanity checks and, for a figure above 30, the evidence for each condition of its exception. The own 5-year median P/E: the "Historical PE 5Years" figure in `<run>/universe.csv`, or computed from year-end P/Es in screener's ratios table (`scr/ratios.json`) (say which). Name the hand-picked peers with their 5-year median P/Es, found the same way. Say whether a re-rating is assumed and the forward evidence it rests on; without that evidence the sheet holds the exit P/E at or below the live P/E.
10. **Evidence quality** (High / Medium / Low), graded per `/principles/data_rules.md`.
11. **Thesis markers** meeting the exit rules and the thesis-marker standard in `/principles/sector_rules.md`. Each {id, level ("company"/"sector"), on ("revenue" / "visibility" / "other": what the marker tracks), text, metric, op, threshold (a number, or [lo, hi] with op "between"; % metrics in percent), event (for metric "event"), due (YYYY-MM-DD), by (true when the line is to be reached by the due date; absent or false when it must hold every quarter until then), check ("script" when `thesis.py check` supports the metric, else "manual"), source, sector_marker_id (sector level)}; the supported metrics and how each is computed are in `scripts/thesis.py check --help`, and `thesis.py register` refuses markers that break its rules (`thesis.py register --help`).
12. **Bent rules** (the bent-rule tier), **thesis**, **kill condition** and **review date**.
13. **Status:**
    - **A company not held:** `list` when it passes every entry test that doesn't depend on price (the non-negotiables, the earnings evidence, a visibility test); the sheet then decides live whether its price clears the hurdle, and shows it on the watchlist with its buy-below price when it doesn't. `watch` when the thesis is waiting on a dated piece of evidence: say what is awaited, by when, the evidence so far and the next check date. Otherwise `reject`.
    - **A holding:** `hold` or `exit`, by the exit rules' one exit test. Say whether the thesis is intact, rewritten or broken; a weak quarter, a price move or a high valuation alone is never a break. A broken thesis is judged as a new candidate at the price in `scr/overview.json`: `hold` on the rewritten thesis if it passes, else `exit`. Every exit says whether it is urgent, with the dated event behind it.
14. **Sector notes:** if this company shows a sector note in `/principles/investing_rules.md` is wrong or missing, propose the change (old and new wording, reason, evidence). Don't edit the rules file.
