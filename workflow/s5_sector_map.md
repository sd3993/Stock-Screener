# S5: sector map (deep-tier subagent; full mode: build or review; recheck mode: marker review)

You build or review the sector map to the standards in `/principles/sector_rules.md`. Write only the files named below. Working directory: the project root (PowerShell). Your brief gives the run folder and the mode.

## Read first
`/principles/sector_rules.md`, `/principles/investing_rules.md` the news and events, sector preferences and use of history rules (what counts as news; the user's sector preferences; what the past may be used for), the run's `sector_clusters.csv` and `sector_stage.csv` (S4), the previous full run's `sector_map.md` and `sectors.json` if any, and the latest `python -I scripts/sectors.py score` output (past calls and how they fared; the orchestrator puts it in `<run>/sector_calls_score.md`).

## Emerging scan (full mode, before the clusters are read)
Search for emerging sectors and businesses first, so the search isn't steered by what the screens already caught:
1. **Problems:** from official sources only (the sector standard's forward-evidence sources, including the Economic Survey, regulators' consultation papers, draft policies and trade statistics on import dependence), list the bottlenecks that have to be solved, each with its figure, date and source.
2. **Companies:** for each problem, find the listed companies working on it: search the saved exchange announcements (`data/companies/*/scr/announcements.json`) for the problem's keywords as well as the news (a web search or screener's search is only a pointer) and confirm each from its own exchange filings (pilot orders, approvals, capex, a new segment line). Classify each per the sector standard: an emerging company (all or nearly all of its business) or a participant (an established company with an emerging business). Check that a scheme's eligibility covers what the company actually makes before counting it as solving the problem. Source the proven demand behind the problem, and say whether the problem is unsolved. Test every problem listed before the map is final; none stays untested. Before an emerging entry is final, run the keyword search over every company under the problem's industry labels, so no company is left "not assessed".
3. **Not priced in:** apply the sector standard's emerging tests. Until the entry's stage numbers exist (`sector_stage.csv` with `--sectors`), judge the stage from the companies' own valuation, price strength and institutional-holding change.
4. Each one that passes becomes an entry marked emerging, its emerging companies as overrides and its participants under `participants` (each participant also needs an override in its main business's entry where its label doesn't map it there; with no such entry its main sector is `other`). A newly notified policy whose emerging test fails still gets a non-emerging entry when a listed company is in the business. List in your reply the problems you looked at and dropped, with the test each failed.

## Build
Give an entry to every sector the sector standard names, with every item it requires, in `sector_map.md` in plain English and in `sectors.json`; every other queued company falls under "other".
- **Stages:** write a draft `sectors.json` with each entry's industries and overrides, run `python -I scripts/sectors.py stage --sectors <run>/sectors.json`, and set each stage from its sector row (with a reason wherever you differ). Re-run it after the last override or participant change, and quote figures only from that last run.
- **Supply balance:** each entry gets its supply balance (from official or filed capacity against demand, with the date a surplus is expected to clear; one criterion across entries, so import-dependent output sold at import parity with no evidence of full plants is balanced, not tight), and its rank follows the sector standard's rank rule.
- **Evidence:** open every cited document and confirm the figure is in it (cite the document that holds the figure, not a release announcing it); a budget line is read with its revised estimate and actual release beside its budget estimate.
- **Markers:** thresholds are set against the last year's official actual, and no "by" marker may already be met when the call is made. Before setting a marker on a company event, read that company's latest results filing and call transcript: a marker already met, or one the company itself guides past the due date, tests nothing. A marker whose date comes only from a rating agency or the news says so in its text. Check each event marker against the filings made up to the call date, not only up to the build. An emerging entry's markers are manual ones on the emerging business itself, not sector medians, which measure the companies' other businesses.

## Suppliers (full mode, after the entries are drafted)
For each focus sector (`/principles/sector_rules.md`), list what its growth needs (materials, components, equipment, services, testing and certification); find the listed makers of each from the saved company descriptions (`data/companies/*/scr/overview.json` "about"), the saved announcements and the news as a pointer, whatever their industry label; run `python -I scripts/sectors.py who` on the candidates and test the share only for those it says would join the queue (the rest are listed with its status); a company whose product is the entry's own output (a named sub-sector) is a member, given an override, never a supplier; confirm each supplier's focus share (an order-book share counts firm orders only, letters of intent out, and overstates a segment when the other segments book and bill within the period) from its filings (segment reporting, the annual report's customer or end-market split, the call transcript) and list those that meet the share test under the entry's `suppliers`. Reply with the inputs looked at and the companies dropped for a small share.

## Upcoming themes (full mode)
List the themes per the sector standard, with problem, markers and companies (material or not; search each company's own saved announcements before calling a link unconfirmed); review each existing theme's markers; promote a theme to an entry when its markers are met.

## Review (a full run with a previous map, and recheck mode)
- An entry that covers the same business keeps its id, whatever its name becomes: the call log and its score follow the id.
- For each existing entry, check every marker due by now: "script" ones from `<run>/marker_check.csv` (`thesis.py check --sectors`), "manual" ones against their named source. Record each state (held / broken / not yet due) with the date and evidence in `sectors.json`.
- A broken marker: rewrite the entry (or mark the tailwind ended), and put the sector and the reason at the top of your reply; the orchestrator tells the user, marks the sector's companies for re-analysis, and in recheck mode proposes a full run for that sector.
- Recheck mode: review only markers that are due or whose script state changed; add no new sector unless a major policy was notified (say so in your reply: it triggers a full run).
- Full-run review: also apply the entry criteria again (the emerging scan included; an emerging entry whose stage has reached recognised loses the mark) and update stages from the new S4 numbers. State in `sector_map.md` how the last calls fared (from the score file).

## Output
- `<run>/sector_map.md`: one section per sector with the items above and their sources (its supply balance and suppliers included); the upcoming themes; then the order of work (sectors by rank with their queued companies and holdings), the emerging entries with the problems looked at and dropped, the past calls' hit rate, and open questions.
- `<run>/sectors.json` (python, ensure_ascii=False): `{"as_of": date, "run": "<FYyyQn>", "sectors": [{"id", "name", "industries", "sub_sectors", "overrides": {sym: reason}, "kind", "emerging" (true/false), "problem" (emerging only: {"text", "value", "date", "source", "url"}), "demand" (emerging only: {"text", "value", "date", "source", "url"}), "unsolved" (emerging only: true/false, with the reason in "problem" text), "participants" (emerging only: {sym: "main sector id: filed evidence of the emerging business"}), "scope": {"reach": "domestic/global", "evidence": [..]}, "supply": {"balance": "tight/balanced/surplus, or n/a only where no capacity measure exists (a service sector)", "clears_by" (surplus only), "evidence": [..]}, "suppliers": {sym: {"supplies", "share", "basis" (filed/estimate), "evidence"}}, "evidence": [{"claim", "value", "date", "source", "url"}], "start", "end", "duration_why", "stage", "stage_reason", "capture", "capture_evidence", "headwinds": [..], "ends_if", "markers": [{"id", "text", "metric", "where", "op", "threshold", "event", "due", "by", "check", "state", "state_date", "state_evidence"}], "rank", "call_date", "changed"}], "upcoming": [{"id", "name", "problem": {"text", "value", "date", "source", "url"}, "markers": [same format as entry markers], "companies": {sym: {"link", "material" (true/false), "evidence"}}}]}`.

Then the orchestrator runs `python -I scripts/sectors.py order` (writes `<run>/sector_of.tsv`, rebuilds the gate batches not yet run to the sector focus and queues the emerging entries' companies and the suppliers) and `python -I scripts/sectors.py themes` (writes the theme watches) and `python -I scripts/sectors.py log` (logs every new or changed call in `runs/sector_calls.csv`, and the marker states in `runs/sector_marker_states.csv`).
