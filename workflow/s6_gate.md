# S6.0: gate, one batch (deep tier; full mode)

Decide NEXT, WATCH or REJECT for each company in your batch, from its figures, its latest guidance and its last 12 months of exchange filings, to the gate standard in `/principles/filter_rules.md`. Do not edit any existing file except a company's `index.json` when you download a filing; list any contrary searches you ran in your report, and the orchestrator logs them. Working directory: the project root (PowerShell). Your brief gives the run folder, the batch number and whether the data was pre-fetched by script.

## Read first
`/principles/filter_rules.md`, `/principles/investing_rules.md` (the non-negotiables, the cash and debt, government customers, earnings evidence, valuation and return math, and news and events rules, and the holding period and hurdle rule), `/principles/data_rules.md`, `/sources/SOURCES.md` and `/sources/screener.md`, the run's `sector_map.md` entries for the sectors in your batch (`sector_of.tsv`, from `sectors.py order`, gives each company's sector), and earlier decisions in `<run>/gate/b*.json` for calibration.

## Per company (write its file before starting the next)
1. **Data:** (a file you download goes into its `<FYyyQn>/` folder and `index.json`, never loose in the company folder)
   - The company's row in `<run>/triage.csv` (it holds the screen fields, including "Pledged percentage").
   - Its screener answers in `data/companies/<SYM>/scr/`, fetched with no model tokens by `uvx --from screener-mcp==0.5.0 python scripts/scr_company.py <SYM>` (the orchestrator runs it for the batch; run it yourself for any company it missed; if screener.in can't be reached: `/sources/screener.md`):
     - statements (12 years, consolidated and standalone);
     - the last 13 quarters;
     - shareholding for the last 12 quarters;
     - NSE announcements for 3 years, with links;
     - the annual-report and transcript list;
     - insider trades.
   - Promoter holding and pledge per quarter come from `scr/shp_history.json` (the filed shareholding-pattern XBRLs).
2. **Sector:** confirm the company's sector from what it actually does (`scr/overview.json` about text, the business description in the documents you read in step 3). If `sector_of.tsv` has it wrong, record the right sector (an id from `sectors.json`, or "other") and why. State whether the confirmed sector puts the company in the focus set or the reserve by `/principles/sector_rules.md`'s focus test, never by the sector's rank.
3. **Guidance:** read the latest earnings-call transcript and investor presentation filed in the last 12 months (download from the exchange links in the saved document list or announcements into `data/companies/<SYM>/<FYyyQn>/`, with text via `pdf_text.py`, recorded in `index.json`); if neither exists, the latest annual report's Management Discussion & Analysis or the AGM speech. Record every numeric guidance item with its timeline (revenue growth, margin, profit, capacity and commissioning dates, order book and inflow, capex, debt) and management's explanation for any weak metric. Which guidance counts: the gate standard. A company within about 5 points of the line gets its latest transcript read end to end, never keyword-scanned.
4. **Filings (news check):** read every rating rationale on file for the company and its promoter group, Issuer Not Cooperating ones included, matching each rating action to its Reg 30 filing (an unfiled adverse action and an unfiled non-adverse one are recorded apart; for a recent listing, read the agencies' history tables back past the listing date and compare any Issuer Not Cooperating rating with what the offer document says; where a rationale cites delays, check the auditor's CARO statement and the offer document for the same months and record whether they capture them), and search the group's rating histories for older defaults the 3-year announcement feed misses; for every listing in the last 3 years, apply the selling test to the IPO's offer for sale (offer document cover), not only to post-listing insider and takeover-code filings; call transcripts are filed under the "Analysts/Institutional Investor Meet/Con. Call Updates" category, so no announcement filter drops it; read a scanned or OCR'd figure from the page image before logging a conflict; open every order filing (the values sit in its annexure; under SEBI's Jan 2026 master circular some give only size bands: record those as headline figures, and net cancelled awards out of the order book), not just its title; for a foreign regulator's action, read the regulator's own decision rather than the company's Reg 30 summary, compare its scope and adverse findings with the company's wording, and time each disclosure against the order date and the first press report; read insider-trading violation reports for trade dates against adverse events; record every settlement or consent order's charges, who settled, the amounts, any debarment and whether it was disclosed; search SEBI's own orders listing (adjudication penalties don't reach the NSE circulars, which start Nov 2023) and the last three annual reports' secretarial audit and strictures sections, and regulator enforcement orders and NSE investigation circulars (they annex SEBI and SAT orders; read the circulars feed a month at a time, keep the investigation department's INVG circulars, curl each PDF from archives.nseindia.com and search the full text) by company and every promoter-group holder's name, and open every orders-type Reg 30 filing across the full 3-year feed; open the reason line of every key-personnel and auditor exit, checking files already on disk before logging a reason as unread; go through the last 12 months of the saved announcements (`scr/announcements.json`); open any filing whose title signals governance or a business event and read it. Record governance (auditor resignation/qualification/change with the stated reason, SEBI/ED/tax/GST actions, fraud or forensic audit, defaults or rating downgrades, promoter sales or purchases, pledge creation/release/invocation, preferential issues/QIPs/warrants, KMP or independent-director exits with the reason, related-party approvals, penalties, plant shutdowns, litigation) and business events (order wins with value, capacity commissioning, acquisitions, rating upgrades). When the promoter stake fell 0.5 point or more in the last 2 quarters or a filing shows a promoter sale, also read the insider trades (`scr/insider.json`) and SAST disclosures (`scr/sast.json`, from NSE; BSE for a per-person annexure) and find the reason (the promoter selling and buying rule), naming every seller from the SAST per-person annexure (rendered when image-only; the insider feed can show only the largest seller's leg) or by diffing the holder lists in the shareholding-pattern XBRLs (names in the `D_` context, share counts in the same context without the prefix; a new public holder matching a promoter's fall marks an off-market transfer; an empty NSE insider feed is no proof of no disclosure), reading the next call transcript (the company-site copy where the exchange holds only a cover letter) before calling it unread. Check the promoter track record lightly (the promoter track record rule): deposits and advances in the related-party balances, each compared with the rent it secures and the rent paid before it, with any loans from the same party; related-party loans traced to their origin in the offer document and settled across the standalone and consolidated s.186(4) tables, the loanee table and CARO 3(iii), with issue money checked against the monitoring agency's table of where unused proceeds sit; a pledge filed only through the depositories' system confirmed per holder in the shareholding XBRL and BSE's pattern, its purpose recorded as unfiled; (with currency translation split from new lending before calling a balance growing); the group companies in the related-party list for defaults (an IBBI title search on every promoter-group name, the list taken from a listed group parent's results annexure where there is one), insolvency, SEBI or ED orders and downgrades, and the latest monitoring agency report for any deviation in the use of issue proceeds; where a past default or restructuring shows, read IBBI's order list by the company's CIN and check each admission date against both exchanges for a same-day disclosure (the 3-year announcement feed misses older events); a poor record is an integrity flag. For a subsidiary's insolvency, the Reg 30 letters' timestamps, the notes on loans to the subsidiary and the guarantee filings answer the rule's tests.
5. **Promoter group below 50%:** for a NEXT, check the parent's pledges, debt and rating from exchange filings.
6. **Recent listings and promoter sales:** for a recent IPO, fetch the SEBI abridged prospectus (restated cash flow, certified order book, litigation summary) and read the RHP's key-parameters footnotes, its LC table and contingent-liability summary (an LC series not shown as contingent is an upper bound on acceptances until the split is found), its restated payables note with its ageing, its certified borrowings table and its working-capital funding table; after the issue, walk equity to find unpaid issue expenses and offer-for-sale money owed to sellers (the monitoring agency's issue-account footnote) inside the liabilities change and record CFO with and without them; judge issue money parked outside the offer document's instruments (not a Reg 32 deviation by itself) by size, duration and whether the monitoring agency disclosed it; rebuild the offer's composition (fresh issue, market-maker reservation, offer for sale) from the offer document and the shareholding pattern before calling it unreconciled, check cash-flow share proceeds against allotments times price, read the offer size, the offer-for-sale part and who sold in it from the RHP cover page (a cash flow showing only the fresh-issue proceeds doesn't rule out an offer for sale), and apply the promoter selling rule, recording one row per half-year (period, profit, CFO, CFO/PAT, the year with each adjacent half, the same half a year earlier, the sale size as % of equity) and listing and sizing the adverse filings after each sale, including its cash leg (look for the company's own quarterly cash-flow slide first; where only half-year cash flows are filed and the test turns on quarters, split them with the company's reported net debt after reconciling to the filed statement and checking its definition holds across the quarters, else settle on the filed half-years; use filed CFO, never a deck's reclassified figure): "results" means profit and operating cash flow together, never profit alone. Compare the RHP's named object assets (a land parcel with its seller and price, quoted machinery) with each monitoring-agency report and the filings since listing: a change needs its Reg 30 disclosure and, for a contract the prospectus refers to, shareholder approval (Companies Act s.27); the agency's "no deviation" doesn't settle the promoter track record. Read the company's Reg 32 statements of deviation or variation (often only on its website) against the agency's narrative and the board resolutions it cites, compare the DRHP's object assets with the RHP's, and find where the object money sits (capitalised, or held as a capital advance) in the latest balance sheet and cash flow.
7. **Always record**, even when another floor decides: 3-year CFO/PAT (from the latest annual report's restated prior years where it restates them, e.g. bank trade credits moved to financing, never mixing restated and unrestated years; the change in acceptances and supply-chain finance moved to financing; match a series renamed across annual reports, e.g. acceptances shown as supplier finance; where the two are filed in separate notes, settle whether one sits inside the other before restating (the ordering of the balances, a rating agency's debt reconciliation and its facility list) and record the answer; where one half-year's restated CFO is already negative, record the rise in acceptances that would turn the other negative too; for a cash-conversion reject, record the fall in acceptances that would lift the ratio to the line) and the latest half-year's CFO, the net working-capital days the cash conversion rule tests (screener's "Cash Conversion Cycle" row in `scr/ratios.json`, not "Working Capital Days") for each of those years, and net debt/equity both without and with leases, to two decimals from the filed balance sheet (above 0.80 is over the line; lien-marked deposits are not cash, nor are IPO deposits earmarked for the issue's objects (except issue money for repaying counted debt, netted against it as the cash and debt rule sets), which can sit inside cash and cash equivalents (compare the cash-flow statement's closing cash and the monitoring agency's deployment table); where a half-year cash flow shows nil tax paid, check whether advance tax is netted inside other current assets (that line's movement against the balance-sheet change) before calling CFO pre-tax; a sale-and-leaseback is financing, so its lease counts as debt). When other financial or other current liabilities jump inside CFO (identify the inflow) while payables fall, test finance cost against borrowings and the payables ageing, and record net debt/equity as if the jump were acceptances. Where a rating agency's gearing implies debt beyond the balance sheet's, reconcile it across every rationale on file (bills discounted, LC-backed creditors) before it decides the debt floor. Before calling a lender undisclosed, read the annual report's borrowings note for loans sanctioned but not yet drawn. For every company heading for NEXT, and whenever finance cost is far above what the borrowings explain, read the annual report's trade-payables note and the latest half-year's, record usance-LC and acceptance amounts and count them in net debt/equity, as the debt rule says.
8. **Return math** at the three projection lengths, as the gate standard sets it (for a manufacturer bounded by capacity, record the revenue its funded capacity can earn against the revenue the g needs at each length, and hold every company to the same test; build revenue capacity from filed production and dated, funded additions; where no capacity figure is filed, build labelled proxies from filed figures (workforce, floor area, asset turnover); never use a projection length whose g needs revenue above them, and record est_cagr at a length the proxy allows; check a proxy against the current run-rate and the full turnover history; growth g, exit P/E, one-offs normalised, including those netted inside expense lines or other income, read from the results notes; associates' contribution is consolidated PAT less standalone PAT, not the gross share line (consolidation taxes it); its own lines for emerging companies and participants); say how you chose g and the exit P/E (est_cagr starts from the normalised P/E the entry states; a trailing period taxed far below the regime rate, from a regime-switch or earlier-years credit or a nil-tax quarter, is re-taxed at the regime rate; where the share count rose, the P/E is market cap on the latest count over trailing profit), record the share count behind the EPS (consolidated net of group-held shares, the shareholding total, diluted for warrants and allotted convertibles, board-approved issues noted) and test the decision on the diluted count; for a cyclical, mid-cycle earnings are the average profit per unit over a full cycle (EBITDA per tonne for metals and miners; an average margin on volume-driven revenue otherwise) times current volumes, taxed and per diluted share; compare mid-cycle and trailing margins on the same EBITDA basis (other income in or out on both), and normalise segment one-offs from the filed segment split of the write-off; a cyclical counts as above mid-cycle when its trailing margin (or per-unit profit) is more than about 1 point above that full-cycle mean; on the capped base g is the volume growth capacity and guidance allow, with margin gains stripped, and the exit P/E bound is re-read on the capped P/E; a full cycle needs a filed trough and peak, and where the company's own history is short, sub-scale or covers one leg, compare steady-scale peers' margins over the same years to tell the cycle from a company-specific change (scale, mix); record each mid-cycle base with its window and the g its capacity allows at the shortest length, using a demerged company's or recent listing's predecessor segment history before calling the history missing, and whether a dated sector tailwind shows in the company's own filings. For a recent listing, the exit P/E comes from the peers' 5-year medians computed from `triage.csv` ("Historical PE 5Years"), noting how long each peer has been listed, not a judged figure. Recompute every sensitivity you quote, and say why one that crosses the line isn't the base case. Record the basis of any guided margin (other income in or out; segment sales or total income) and whether stated operating leverage is inside it.
9. **Competitors:** for a company heading for NEXT or WATCH in a competitive sector or stage (the filter standard; the entry's `supply.by_stage` in `sectors.json` gives each stage's balance and capture, and a company selling in several stages is split by its filed revenue or output share), record its named rivals with their sales growth and margins over the same periods (for a company in several segments, the segment that puts it in the sector, against the peers' figures), and its own evidence of beating them. A stage's or group's first peer set takes every listed company `sectors.json` names for it (`by_stage` companies, overrides, suppliers), batch members included, fetching any missing. A group's peer set holds the listed companies whose main business (the largest filed revenue share summed by sector, from the business-line revenue table, in each year of the medians' windows) is the group's business, plus any company with a filed segment for it, measured on that segment on the set's basis (a segment mixing groups or filed on another basis is a bound, not entered); the method and membership test are fixed for the run, not the list: a member whose main business is confirmed elsewhere leaves every set it sat in on entity figures and joins its new group's sets, and every entry measured on a changed set is re-based in the next rule-change round. Every company in one competitive group is measured against the same peer set (the set the group's earlier entries recorded), the company is left out of its own median, and the median is recorded for each period the growth claim uses. Where return on capital stands in for the margin, it is operating profit less depreciation (other income and fair-value gains out) over average equity, reserves and borrowings (leases included), a half-year doubled over the latest balance-sheet date every peer has, the same for every company.
10. **Decide** NEXT, WATCH or REJECT on the gate standard's line, price watch, growth watch and floors. For a growth watch tested, record the CAGR without the drag at every length that reaches the marker, name the best, and record the EBITDA or EPS the line needs.
11. **Verify before rejecting:** every figure a REJECT rests on is checked against the filed document, per `/principles/data_rules.md`. A NEXT with a promoter fall of 0.5 point or more starts its "why" with "check promoter:".

- `<run>/gate/bNN.json`: a list, one entry per company: {sym, decision ("NEXT"/"WATCH"/"REJECT"), why (one sentence with the deciding numbers), g_used, exit_pe_used, est_cagr (at the best projection length; null for an emerging company with no numeric basis), years (2, 5 or 7: that length), floor_failed (or null), verified_against (document and page, for every REJECT); for a WATCH also watch_kind ("price" or "growth") and waiting_for (one line: the price or earnings rise that would bring it back; for a growth watch, the drag and the marker that ends it); a price watch also buy_below (₹), eps_basis (the EPS the buy-below is set on) and eps_basis_kind ("trailing": the normalised trailing EPS the P/E used; "adjusted_trailing": trailing with one-offs taken out that the reported trailing figure still holds; "mid_cycle": a mid-cycle EPS); a growth watch also marker_due (YYYY-MM-DD, the marker's date) and cagr_without_drag}.
