# S6.0: gate, one batch (deep tier; full mode)

Decide NEXT, WATCH or REJECT for each company in your batch, from its figures, its latest guidance and its last 12 months of exchange filings, to the gate standard in `/principles/filter_rules.md`. Do not edit any existing file except a company's `index.json` when you download a filing; list any contrary searches you ran in your report, and the orchestrator logs them. Working directory: the project root (PowerShell). Your brief gives the run folder, the batch number and whether the data was pre-fetched by script.

## Read first
`/principles/filter_rules.md`, `/principles/investing_rules.md` (the non-negotiables, the cash and debt, government customers, earnings evidence, valuation and return math, and news and events rules, and the holding period and hurdle rule), `/principles/data_rules.md`, `/sources/SOURCES.md` and `/sources/screener.md`, the run's `sector_map.md` entries for the sectors in your batch (`sector_of.tsv`, from `sectors.py order`, gives each company's sector), and earlier decisions in `<run>/gate/b*.json` for calibration.

## Per company (write its file before starting the next)
1. **Data:** (a file you download goes into its `<FYyyQn>/` folder and `index.json`, never loose in the company folder)
   - The company's row in `<run>/triage.csv` (it holds the screen fields, including "Pledged percentage").
   - Its screener answers in `data/companies/<SYM>/scr/`, fetched with no model tokens by `uvx --from screener-mcp==0.5.0 python scripts/scr_company.py <SYM>` (the orchestrator runs it for the batch; run it yourself for any company it missed; if screener.in can't be reached: `/sources/screener.md`):
     - statements (12 years, consolidated and standalone);
     - the last 13 quarters;
     - shareholding for the last 12 quarters;
     - NSE announcements for 3 years, with links;
     - the annual-report and transcript list;
     - insider trades.
   - Promoter holding and pledge per quarter come from `scr/shp_history.json` (the filed shareholding-pattern XBRLs).
2. **Sector:** confirm the company's sector from what it actually does (`scr/overview.json` about text, the business description in the documents you read in step 3). If `sector_of.tsv` has it wrong, record the right sector (an id from `sectors.json`, or "other") and why.
3. **Guidance:** read the latest earnings-call transcript and investor presentation filed in the last 12 months (download from the exchange links in the saved document list or announcements into `data/companies/<SYM>/<FYyyQn>/`, with text via `pdf_text.py`, recorded in `index.json`); if neither exists, the latest annual report's Management Discussion & Analysis or the AGM speech. Record every numeric guidance item with its timeline (revenue growth, margin, profit, capacity and commissioning dates, order book and inflow, capex, debt) and management's explanation for any weak metric. Which guidance counts: the gate standard. A company within about 5 points of the line gets its latest transcript read end to end, never keyword-scanned.
4. **Filings (news check):** search the promoter group companies' rating histories for older defaults the 3-year announcement feed misses; read a scanned or OCR'd figure from the page image before logging a conflict; open every order filing (the values sit in its annexure; under SEBI's Jan 2026 master circular some give only size bands: record those as headline figures, and net cancelled awards out of the order book), not just its title; open the reason line of every key-personnel and auditor exit, checking files already on disk before logging a reason as unread; go through the last 12 months of the saved announcements (`scr/announcements.json`); open any filing whose title signals governance or a business event and read it. Record governance (auditor resignation/qualification/change with the stated reason, SEBI/ED/tax/GST actions, fraud or forensic audit, defaults or rating downgrades, promoter sales or purchases, pledge creation/release/invocation, preferential issues/QIPs/warrants, KMP or independent-director exits with the reason, related-party approvals, penalties, plant shutdowns, litigation) and business events (order wins with value, capacity commissioning, acquisitions, rating upgrades). When the promoter stake fell 0.5 point or more in the last 2 quarters or a filing shows a promoter sale, also read the insider trades (`scr/insider.json`) and SAST disclosures (`scr/sast.json`, from NSE; BSE for a per-person annexure) and find the reason (the promoter selling and buying rule). Check the promoter track record lightly (the promoter track record rule): the group companies in the related-party list for defaults, insolvency, SEBI or ED orders and downgrades, and the latest monitoring agency report for any deviation in the use of issue proceeds; where a past default or restructuring shows, read IBBI's order list by the company's CIN and check each admission date against both exchanges for a same-day disclosure (the 3-year announcement feed misses older events); a poor record is an integrity flag. For a subsidiary's insolvency, the Reg 30 letters' timestamps, the notes on loans to the subsidiary and the guarantee filings answer the rule's tests.
5. **Promoter group below 50%:** for a NEXT, check the parent's pledges, debt and rating from exchange filings.
6. **Recent listings and promoter sales:** for a recent IPO, fetch the SEBI abridged prospectus (restated cash flow, certified order book, litigation summary) and read the RHP's restated payables note with its ageing, its certified borrowings table and its working-capital funding table; read the offer size, the offer-for-sale part and who sold in it, and apply the promoter selling rule, including its cash leg: "results" means profit and operating cash flow together, never profit alone.
7. **Always record**, even when another floor decides: 3-year CFO/PAT (from the latest annual report's restated prior years where it restates them, e.g. bank trade credits moved to financing, never mixing restated and unrestated years; the change in acceptances and supply-chain finance moved to financing; match a series renamed across annual reports, e.g. acceptances shown as supplier finance; where the two are filed in separate notes, settle whether one sits inside the other before restating (the ordering of the balances, a rating agency's debt reconciliation and its facility list) and record the answer; where one half-year's restated CFO is already negative, record the rise in acceptances that would turn the other negative too; for a cash-conversion reject, record the fall in acceptances that would lift the ratio to the line) and the latest half-year's CFO, the net working-capital days the cash conversion rule tests (screener's "Cash Conversion Cycle" row in `scr/ratios.json`, not "Working Capital Days") for each of those years, and net debt/equity both without and with leases, to two decimals from the filed balance sheet (above 0.80 is over the line; lien-marked deposits are not cash; a sale-and-leaseback is financing, so its lease counts as debt). Before calling a lender undisclosed, read the annual report's borrowings note for loans sanctioned but not yet drawn. For every company heading for NEXT, and whenever finance cost is far above what the borrowings explain, read the annual report's trade-payables note and the latest half-year's, record usance-LC and acceptance amounts and count them in net debt/equity, as the debt rule says.
8. **Return math** at the three projection lengths, as the gate standard sets it (for a manufacturer bounded by capacity, record the revenue its funded capacity can earn against the revenue the g needs at each length, and hold every company to the same test; where no capacity figure is filed, build labelled proxies from filed figures (workforce, floor area, asset turnover); never use a projection length whose g needs revenue above them, and record est_cagr at a length the proxy allows; check a proxy against the current run-rate and the full turnover history; growth g, exit P/E, one-offs normalised, including those netted inside expense lines or other income, read from the results notes; its own lines for emerging companies and participants); say how you chose g and the exit P/E (est_cagr starts from the normalised P/E the entry states; a trailing period taxed far below the regime rate, from a regime-switch or earlier-years credit or a nil-tax quarter, is re-taxed at the regime rate; where the share count rose, the P/E is market cap on the latest count over trailing profit), record the share count behind the EPS (consolidated net of group-held shares, the shareholding total, diluted for warrants and allotted convertibles, board-approved issues noted) and test the decision on the diluted count; for a cyclical, mid-cycle earnings are the average profit per unit over a full cycle (EBITDA per tonne for metals and miners; an average margin on volume-driven revenue otherwise) times current volumes, taxed and per diluted share; record each mid-cycle base with its window and the g its capacity allows at the shortest length, using a demerged company's or recent listing's predecessor segment history before calling the history missing, and whether a dated sector tailwind shows in the company's own filings. For a recent listing, the exit P/E comes from the peers' 5-year medians computed from `triage.csv` ("Historical PE 5Years"), noting how long each peer has been listed, not a judged figure. Record the basis of any guided margin (other income in or out; segment sales or total income) and whether stated operating leverage is inside it.
9. **Competitors:** for a company heading for NEXT or WATCH in a competitive sector (the filter standard), record its named rivals with their sales growth and margins over the same periods (for a company in several segments, the segment that puts it in the sector, against the peers' figures), and its own evidence of beating them. Every company in one competitive group is measured against the same peer set for the whole run (the set the group's earlier entries recorded), the company is left out of its own median, and the median is recorded for each period the growth claim uses. Where return on capital stands in for the margin, it is operating profit less depreciation (other income and fair-value gains out) over average equity, reserves and borrowings (leases included), a half-year doubled over the latest balance-sheet date every peer has, the same for every company.
10. **Decide** NEXT, WATCH or REJECT on the gate standard's line, price watch, growth watch and floors. For a growth watch tested, record the CAGR without the drag at every length that reaches the marker, name the best, and record the EBITDA or EPS the line needs.
11. **Verify before rejecting:** every figure a REJECT rests on is checked against the filed document, per `/principles/data_rules.md`. A NEXT with a promoter fall of 0.5 point or more starts its "why" with "check promoter:".

- `<run>/gate/bNN.json`: a list, one entry per company: {sym, decision ("NEXT"/"WATCH"/"REJECT"), why (one sentence with the deciding numbers), g_used, exit_pe_used, est_cagr (at the best projection length; null for an emerging company with no numeric basis), years (2, 5 or 7: that length), floor_failed (or null), verified_against (document and page, for every REJECT); for a WATCH also watch_kind ("price" or "growth") and waiting_for (one line: the price or earnings rise that would bring it back; for a growth watch, the drag and the marker that ends it); a price watch also buy_below (₹) and eps_basis (the normalised trailing EPS the P/E used); a growth watch also marker_due (YYYY-MM-DD, the marker's date) and cagr_without_drag}.
