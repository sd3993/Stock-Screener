# Expert evidence on Indian multibaggers (task 2, step 6)

Written 3 Nov 2026 for `plan.md` step 6. It tests `process/investing_rules.md` (rule numbers below are that file's) against what named practitioners and researchers found about past Indian multibaggers and wealth creators. Tags follow the rules file: **[Expert: name, work]**. Opinion pieces and anonymous websites are not used.

**How to read each finding**
- **Type:** *Evidence* (a data study with a stated sample) or *Opinion* (a practitioner's framework or stated belief, sometimes illustrated with examples).
- **Bias flags:**
  - *Survivor sample*: only winners are studied, with no losers or controls.
  - *Hindsight*: the grouping uses information from after the entry date, such as perfect-foresight PEG, buying at the year's low, or classifying on the same years as the returns.
  - *One regime*: the result comes from a single market period.
- **Rule effect:** supports, contradicts or refines a numbered rule.
- Every number below was read in the source named. Market caps in INR billion are also given in ₹ crore (INR 1 bn = ₹100 cr).

## 1. Corrections to wording in the current rules file

These are the places where the rules file cites a source but says something the source doesn't.

**C1. Rule 6 (long term), the growth-persistence statistic is misquoted.**
- The rule says: "of 283 Indian firms growing 25%+ in 2003, only 103 kept it up for 5 years".
- The source says something else. In 2003, 283 companies had PAT ≥ INR 200 mn (₹20 cr). Of these:
  - 103 delivered PAT CAGR ≥25% over 2003-08;
  - only 13 sustained it over 2008-13;
  - 0 sustained it over 2013-18.
- The other cohorts behave the same way: 1998 (188 companies → 28 → 8 → 1), 2001 (241 → 69 → 22 → 1) and 2002 (236 → 91 → 16 → 0). [Expert: Agrawal, MOSL 23rd WCS, Exhibit 16]
- So the persistence rate from one 5-year 25%+ spell to the next is roughly 10-30% (13/103 = 13%; 8/28 = 29%), not 36%. The haircut on long-term guidance should be steeper than the current wording implies.

**C2. Rule 6 (short term), "Indian evidence for post-earnings drift is weak" overstates the case.**
- HSY-2018 (Nifty 500, Q4 2002 to Q3 2017) finds statistically significant drift:
  - Over days +2 to +64, the top-SUE decile earns +3.4% excess and the bottom decile −1.4%, a 4.8% spread significant at 1%.
  - It holds in both sub-periods (split at 2008) and after controls for beta, size, P/B, illiquidity and idiosyncratic volatility.
- GD-2021 (100 NSE firms, 2014-18, 1,130 observations) concludes the market "tends to be efficient" on earnings announcements.
- Fair wording: "Indian evidence on drift is mixed; the largest sample (Nifty 500, 15 years) finds a modest drift of about 5% over three months." Not chasing spikes can stay as a valuation discipline, but not on the grounds that drift is absent.

**C3. Rule 8, the 50x line is supported but is about relative return, not wealth destruction.**
- MOSL-23 (stocks with market cap ≥ INR 10 bn (₹1,000 cr), each year 1998-2015, 4,015 instances), average 3-year alpha versus the Sensex by trailing P/E band at entry:

| Trailing P/E | 3-year alpha | Instances |
|---|---|---|
| 0-10 | +5% | 945 |
| 10-20 | +1% | 1,385 |
| 20-30 | −2% | 812 |
| 30-40 | −1% | 355 |
| 40-50 | −1% | 167 |
| 50-75 | −8% | 150 |
| >75 | −18% | 201 |

- The study's words are "Clearly, buying richly valued stocks doesn't work, especially P/E of 50x and above."
- Suggested wording: "50x+ entries underperformed the Sensex by 8-18% over the next 3 years on average".
- Regime caveat: the window ends in 2015. The post-2015 tables (finding F17) show many 5-year winners entering at P/E above 30. The 50 cap needs re-testing on 2016-23 starts (H1).

**C4. Rule 9 (MOSL 2020-25 note): checked and accurate.**
- In March 2020 the top-10 fastest wealth creators had market caps of INR 12-151 bn (₹1.2k-15.1k cr).
- Of the 8 with a meaningful P/E, 6 were at 4-17x. The exceptions were FACT at 599x and Dixon at 34x. [MOSL-30, Exhibit 5]

## 2. Short term (~1-2 years)

**F1. Momentum is the strongest systematic factor in India.** *Evidence; one regime per version.*
- Over Jan 1994 to Dec 2013, the WML momentum factor's cumulative log return was 341%, with a mean of 17.0% a year. Over the same period SMB (size) was −58% and HML (value) +216%. [AJV-2014, Table 4]
- The revised abstract (Jan 1994 to Dec 2014) gives average annual returns of: momentum 21.9%, HML 15.3%, SMB "nearly 0%", market excess 11.5%.
- The later series reports WML at 19.18% a year (volatility 16.84%), with a worst drawdown of −48.6% (Mar to Jun 2009, recovered Aug 2011). [AJV-2017]
- Caveat: WML rebalances monthly, so trading costs are higher (paper's footnote).
- Rule effect:
  - Supports rule 11 for the short term (prefer a rising market and sponsorship).
  - Refines rule 6(a): price strength, not only earnings acceleration, carries information at 6-12 months.

**F2. Post-earnings drift exists but is modest.** *Evidence (HSY-2018) against counter-evidence (GD-2021).* See C2.
- Horizon: short (about 3 months).
- Rule effect: contradicts the rule 6 wording as it stands.

**F3. Two-year triplers in a bubble were mostly transitory.** *Evidence; one regime (1998-2003).*
- MOSL-8 took the 115 stocks that more than trebled during 1998-2000, bought on 1 Apr 1998 and sold on 31 Dec 2003.
- In the study's words, most "turned out to be transitory multibaggers. They ended up destroying as much wealth in their journey downwards as they had created during their journey upwards."
- MOSL-8 characterises transitory multibaggers as having "fluctuating and low RoE and RoCE", unimpressive records and a post-peak correction "typically almost 100%". Examples: Pentamedia, NIIT, Silverline, DSQ.
- Rule effect:
  - Supports rule 12 (short-term: sell at target; never add to a damaged thesis).
  - Supports rule 6, which asks for quality (ROE) behind a short-term story.

**F4. Short-horizon booms cluster when earnings yield is high relative to bond yields.** *Evidence (descriptive), one market.*
- MOSL-8 counted two-year triplers with at least INR 1 bn of net wealth created:
  - 1990-92: 118, at earnings yield / G-sec 0.40;
  - 1998-2000: 115, at 0.24, sector-specific;
  - 2002-04: 428, at 1.04;
  - 2003-05 (to Dec 2003): 734, at 1.53.
- In quiet windows there were only 2-20.
- Rule effect: refines rule 11. Short-term multibaggers are a market-regime phenomenon, so the short bucket should expect far more candidates after a de-rating (low market P/E, falling rates).

**F5. In the fastest 5-year runs, about half the return came from P/E re-rating.** *Evidence (my computation from MOSL tables); survivor sample.*
- Re-rating share = ln(P/E end ÷ P/E start) ÷ ln(total multiple).
- Top-10 fastest 2020-25 (7 with valid P/E at both ends): median 0.50. Examples:
  - RVNL: P/E 4 → 57, share 0.80;
  - Hitachi Energy: 16 → 147, share 0.78;
  - Persistent: 13 → 63, share 0.53;
  - Jindal Stainless: 17 → 19, share 0.03.
- Top-10 fastest 2009-14 (9 valid): median about 0.38. Examples:
  - Supreme Industries: P/E 3 → 23, share 0.64;
  - Eicher: 9 → 31, share 0.38;
  - Aurobindo: 10 → 13, share 0.10.
- MOSL-30's own summary is the opinion that "In most fast Wealth Creator cases, returns are a function of both – rapid earnings growth, coupled with expansion in valuations… occasionally… led only by P/E expansion". It adds: "frothy P/Es are highly profitable and unmissable selling opportunities."
- Caveat: the total-return multiples include dividends, so the shares are approximate. [MOSL-19 Exhibit 3; MOSL-30 Exhibit 5]
- Rule effect: refines rule 8. For short and medium winners, re-rating is often the larger engine, so "the re-rating needs a reason" is the right test. A short-term exit P/E capped at 30 would have sold most of these early.

**F6. Crashes produce fast, consistent winners from low-P/E quality.** *Evidence (descriptive); one regime (Covid low).*
- Seven of the 10 most consistent wealth creators of 2020-25 traded at single-digit P/E in March 2020, and five of those seven had ROE above 15% in FY20. [MOSL-30, takeaway #4]
- In March 2020, 6 PSUs traded at trailing P/E below 5x. The 26 PSUs in the top 100 delivered 34% PAT CAGR and 35% price CAGR. [MOSL-30, Exhibits 16-17]
- Rule effect: supports rule 19 (buy after a fall) and rule 6(b) (stalwart rebound at a P/E below its own range).

**F7. Bruised blue chips: big drawdowns, then high returns from the low.** *Evidence with Hindsight (measured from the exact 10-year low).*
- Universe:
  - Blue chips are 10+ years listed and either top 50 by market cap, or top 250 with a 10-year average ROE ≥20%.
  - "Bruised" means the stock fell ≥50% from its 5-year high at some point in the next 10 years.
- Across seven rolling 10-year periods ending 2018-2024:
  - Bruised blue chips fell an average 75% from the 5-year high to the 10-year low, and returned 55% CAGR from that low (period averages range 24-131%).
  - Non-bruised blue chips fell an average 28% and returned 23%.
  - All 99 bruised blue chips "continues to exist".
- At their lows, the 10 examples had a median P/E of 8 (23 at the high) and a median P/B of 0.8 (5.5 at the high).
- Triggers to watch: a sector tailwind or a change of management. Entry: "Price/Book less than 2x". Healing factors include debt reduction, restructuring and new management. [MOSL-29, Exhibits 2-4, 21-22]
- Rule effect:
  - Supports rule 19 and the turnaround checks (rule 2).
  - Refines rule 8: for "Volatiles", P/B against their own history is the valuation anchor rather than P/E (see F23).

## 3. Medium term (~3-5 years)

**F8. Mid-to-Mega: the most likely 5-year route to a multi-bagger.** *Evidence; Survivor sample in the performance table, but the 6-step screen is a forward back-test.*
- Mid-to-Mega means rising from market-cap rank 101-300 into the top 100 within 5 years.
- Over 2000-15, the 11 rolling 5-year Mid-to-Mega portfolios had:
  - median return 46% CAGR (range 29-115%);
  - median alpha over the Sensex 28%;
  - median PAT CAGR 35%;
  - median portfolio ROE 26% over the period and 20% in the purchase year;
  - median P/E at purchase 15 (range 3-23; 15-23 in 2005-15).
- Probability of crossing from Mid to Mega was 5-12% per window. For 2010-15:
  - Mini-to-Mega: 3 of 1,908 (0.2%);
  - Mini-to-Mid: 64 of 1,908 (3.4%);
  - Mid-to-Mega: 24 of 200 (12%).
- The 6-step forward screen:
  1. Start with Mids (rank 101-300).
  2. Purchase-year ROE ≥20%.
  3. Industry leader, or a value-migration beneficiary.
  4. Preceding 2-year PAT CAGR ≥20%.
  5. Secular, not cyclical.
  6. P/E below 25.
- In 2010-15 the screen picked 9 stocks; 4 crossed into the top 100 (Aurobindo, Pidilite, Bajaj Finserv, LIC Housing), and the portfolio averaged 33% versus the Sensex's 10%.
- About 70% of Mid-to-Mega companies were industry leaders (top 3 by revenue). [MOSL-20]
- Rule effect:
  - Supports rule 9 (medium ideally under ~₹25k cr).
  - Supports rule 6/medium (20%+ growth plus quality).
  - Refines rule 8: P/E ≤25 at entry was the medium-term norm.

**F9. The 2020-25 crossover matrix confirms the pattern.** *Evidence; one regime (from the Covid low).*
- Of 750 Minis (ranks 251-1000) in 2020:
  - 1 reached Mega (CG Power, 163% CAGR);
  - 29 reached Mid (68% CAGR);
  - 720 stayed Mini (34%).
- Of 150 Mids, 22 reached Mega (56%), 72 stayed (34%) and 57 slipped to Mini (13%).
- The Nifty 50 TRI returned 24%.
- MOSL's conclusion: "The most potent and focused hunting ground… is the Mid category (rank 101 to 250)", with "12-25 of these stocks (healthy 8-16% strike rate)" expected to cross over. [MOSL-30, Exhibits 18-21]
- Rule effect: supports rule 9 (medium).

**F10. Price at entry: 1998-2015 evidence favours low P/E and low PEG, and penalises 50x+.** *Evidence; the PEG part is Hindsight.*
- P/E bands: see C3.
- P/E relative to market:

| P/E ÷ market P/E | 3-year alpha | Instances |
|---|---|---|
| ≤1 | +4% | 1,984 |
| 2-3 | −5% | 330 |
| >3 | −11% | 313 |

- PEG uses perfect foresight of the next 3 years' PAT:

| PEG | 3-year alpha | Instances |
|---|---|---|
| 0-0.5 | +26% | 633 |
| 0.5-1 | +11% | 677 |
| 1-1.5 | +3% | 393 |
| 1.5-2 | −3% | 227 |
| 2-3 | −4% | 266 |
| 3+ | −10% | 463 |
| <0 (falling PAT) | −19% | 1,356 |

- Single-digit P/E was not consistent: its average alpha was negative in 8 of 18 years. [MOSL-23, Exhibits 19-22]
- Rule effect:
  - Supports rule 8: PEG 0-1 preferred, PEG above 2 or negative avoided, 50x at entry fails.
  - Refines rule 8: the PEG evidence uses realised growth, so its value depends entirely on the EPS forecast (step 2) being right. Negative future growth is the single worst bucket.

**F11. Payback ratio <1x (market cap below the next 5 years' cumulative PAT) is the most reliable marker of the fastest wealth creation.** *Evidence with Hindsight (uses realised future PAT).*
- 5-year alpha by payback ratio, 1998-2013, 3,372 instances:

| Payback ratio | 5-year alpha | Instances |
|---|---|---|
| 0-1 | +17% | 454 |
| 3+ | −12% | 1,153 |
| <0 (cumulative loss) | −26% | 375 |

- Seven of the 10 fastest wealth creators of 2011-16 had payback <1x. [MOSL-23 Exhibit 23; MOSL-21 table]
- MOSL-25 recorded that payback <1 "fails to offer the highest return for the first time ever" in 2015-20.
- Rule effect: supports rule 8's return math (horizon EPS × exit P/E ÷ price). Payback is the same idea expressed as cumulative earnings.

**F12. Emergence: buying when ROE first crosses 15%.** *Evidence; a forward-applicable rule, small sample (17).*
- Screen:
  1. Company under 25 years old.
  2. ROE crosses 15% for the first time.
  3. Minimum PAT.
  4. Favourable parent or management.
  5. Avoid cyclicals.
  6. P/E ≤20 on last reported earnings.
- Over 2001-08 it found 17 companies. In the 5 years after emergence they averaged:
  - PAT CAGR 24% and ROE 32%;
  - price CAGR 41%, outperforming the benchmark by 24%;
  - average P/E at emergence 10 (median 8, from the table).
- Amplifiers:
  - high profit-pool industry: +37% versus +4%;
  - consumer advantage: +31% versus +16% for production advantage;
  - top-3 market position: +28% versus +18%.
- Combining all three gave 65% CAGR and +43% relative.
- Failures in the list: Plastiblends (+4%, −8% relative), KPIT (+4%), Suprajit (0%).
- MOSL also found only 17 successful emergences in 8 years, "less than 2 every year". [MOSL-18, section 2.3]
- Rule effect:
  - Refines rule 2 (trajectory): an improving ROE crossing the cost of equity is a dated, observable start signal.
  - Supports rule 8 (P/E ≤20 at entry).

**F13. Economic profit and hockey-stick returns.** *Evidence; Survivor sample for the 54 hockey-stick names, forward test for the economic-profit portfolios.*
- Definitions:
  - Economic profit (EP) = net worth × (ROE − 10%).
  - A hockey-stick return is a price CAGR of at least 25% over 10 years (2013-23, top 500 by 2013 market cap).
- Forward test of EP portfolios: in 8 of 10 years the EP portfolio beat the market, averaging 8% alpha. The economic-loss portfolio did so in 6 of 10, averaging 2%.
- Starting EP quintile matters (average of six 10-year periods, 2008-23):
  - companies starting in quintile 2 or 3 averaged 19% CAGR;
  - moves from 3 to 1 averaged 33%, 3 to 2 averaged 25%, and 2 to 1 averaged 26%;
  - the Nifty 500 averaged 11%.
- The 54 hockey-stick names, in 2013:
  - 70% had P/E ≤20: 19 at ≤10 and 19 at 10-20;
  - returns by entry band were ≤10x: 36%, 10-20x: 35%, >20x: 28%;
  - 31 (57%) moved up an EP quintile;
  - 44 of the 54 came from market-cap quintiles 3-5 (ranks 201-500): 15% of the 300 there, against 10 of 200 in the top 200 (5%).
- Change of management was a frequent "move" (Britannia 2013, CG Power 2020, JB Chem/KKR 2020, Persistent's new CEO 2020). [MOSL-28, Exhibits 6, 10, 20-21, 25-26]
- Rule effect:
  - Supports rule 9 (smaller start) and rule 8 (P/E ≤20 preferred).
  - Supports rule 6/long (ROE above 20% with reinvestment).

**F14. Earnings growth was the main driver of wealth creation; most wealth came from 25%+ growers.** *Evidence; Survivor sample.*
- In 1998-2003, the 48 of 83 wealth creators with earnings growth above 25% created 78% of the wealth. [MOSL-8]
- In 2020-25, the Wealthex (top 100) outperformed the Sensex, 37% CAGR against 17%. The study attributes this "fully" to EPS growth (30% against 17%), with re-rating of 4-5% for both. [MOSL-30, Exhibit 11]
- Rule effect: supports rule 6/medium.

**F15. Quantum jumps in earnings (turnarounds) are often priced in already.** *Evidence; one regime (2008-13).*
- In 2008-13, nine companies delivered 115% PAT CAGR combined, but their price CAGR was only 18% because P/Es were high in 2008. [MOSL-18, takeaway #8]
- Counter-point: in 2019-24, companies loss-making in 2019 had the highest return among the top 100, at 46% CAGR (14 companies, e.g. Tata Motors, Suzlon, CG Power). [MOSL-29, Exhibit 22]
- Rule effect: refines rule 2's turnaround checks. Turnaround payoffs are regime-dependent; "stock still trough-priced" is the key check.

**F16. Consistents bought below their 10-year median P/E won most of the time.** *Evidence; Nifty stocks since 2005, small sample.*
- A "Consistent" is a company whose PAT fell more than 10% no more than three times in 15 years, never fell more than 50%, and ended above where it started.
- Probability of positive alpha when bought below the 10-year median P/E: HDFC Bank 89%, L&T 83%, Reliance 100%, Titan 96%, UltraTech 91%.
- For Volatiles, the anchor is P/B below its 10-year median: Axis 81%, Maruti 83%, SBI 83%, Tata Steel 71%. Tata Motors was the exception at 38%. [MOSL-27, Exhibit 19]
- Rule effect:
  - Supports rule 8's anchor (own median P/E as the starting exit P/E, and a buy-below price when the hurdle needs a P/E above average).
  - Refines rule 8: for cyclicals or Volatiles, anchor on P/B, not P/E.

**F17. Since about 2016, many 5-year winners started at P/E above 30.** *Evidence; Survivor sample (top 100 winners only); regime result.*
- Number of the top-100 wealth creators by P/E at the start of each 5-year period:

| Start year | P/E <10 | P/E >30 | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2009 | 45 | 8 (band >25) | MOSL-19 |
| 2012 | 19 | 13 | MOSL-22 |
| 2013 | 22 | 21 | MOSL-23 |
| 2014 | 27 | 26 | MOSL-24 |
| 2016 | 12 | 40 | MOSL-26 |
| 2017 | 3 | 45 | MOSL-27 |
| 2018 | 3 | 48 | MOSL-28 |
| 2019 | 13 | 42 | MOSL-29 |
| 2020 | 26 | 22 | MOSL-30 |

- In 2018-23, "most valuation rules [were] thrown out of the window": entries at 25-30x returned 35% CAGR against 15% for entries below 10x. [MOSL-28]
- In 2020-25, "valuations did not matter significantly": below 10x returned 40% against 36% for above 30x. [MOSL-30, Exhibit 22]
- Caveat: the bands stop at ">30", so these tables cannot test a 50x line.
- Rule effect: refines rule 8. The low-P/E advantage is strong in 1998-2015 data and weak in the 2016-24 regime. The 50 cap should be re-tested by regime (H1) before any change.

## 4. Long term (5-10+ years)

**F18. India's enduring 100-baggers, 1994-2014.** *Evidence; Survivor sample and Hindsight (bought at each year's lowest price).*
- 47 stocks rose 100x or more and kept that status at March 2014 prices.
- Average multiple 332x over an average 15 years, which is 47% CAGR, or about 12 years to 100x.
- At purchase:
  - average revenue about INR 3 bn (₹300 cr); only 3 of the 47 had double-digit-billion revenue;
  - average market cap INR 2.5 bn (₹250 cr); only NMDC was in double-digit billions;
  - average P/E about 6; 30 of the 47 were below 5x, 10 at 5-10x, 6 above 10x and 1 loss-making.
- The P/E rose from about 6 to 24 over 12 years (4x), so earnings grew about 25x (31% CAGR). By my computation, re-rating was about 30% of the log return (ln 4 ÷ ln 100).
- Business types: 19 value-migration beneficiaries, 10 dominant players, 10 niche players and 8 commodity plays.
- There were 163 "100x opportunities" over 1994-2009 (stock-years from which 100x was still achieved), against 47 stocks. Many winners offered multi-year buying windows: Motherson and Shree Cement 11 years, Lupin 9.
- MOSL's suggested hunting ground: market cap not "significantly exceeding USD 0.5 bn or INR 30 bn" (₹3,000 cr), with a TTM P/E ≤25 in its 2014 screen. [MOSL-19]
- Rule effect:
  - Supports rule 9/long (ideally under ~₹5k cr).
  - Supports rule 10 (SQGLP).
  - Refines rule 8/long: 100x entries were overwhelmingly at single-digit P/E, far below the 50 cap. The pricing edge in the long bucket came from paying very little, not from "paying more for longevity".
  - The multi-year buying window supports rule 18 (no rush; tranches).

**F19. Hyper-growth rarely lasts beyond 5-6 years.** *Evidence.* See C1.
- MOSL-22 (2,700 companies 1997-2017; 112 with a "growth advantage period" of 5+ years, meaning successive years of rolling 5-year PAT CAGR ≥15% with no annual PAT decline):
  - only 30 of 112 (27%) sustained it beyond 9 years;
  - 24 of those 30 (80%) were secular and 24 had a "high growth mindset";
  - median PAT CAGR during the period was 39% for high-industry-growth plus high-mindset companies, and 25% for low-low.
  - Cyclicals grow faster in short bursts. 13 of the 15 highest-growth companies started with PAT below INR 1 bn (₹100 cr), and 10 below INR 10 bn (₹1,000 cr) market cap.
- Over 1997-2017, only 87 of 613 companies listed throughout had price CAGR above 25%, and 89 had profit CAGR above 25%.
- MOSL-23's advice: "investors already holding stocks… in the hyper growth mode must seriously consider exiting if valuations hit exuberant levels (… 2x Market P/E or 3x PEG whichever is higher…)." [MOSL-22, MOSL-23 §5.4-5.5]
- Rule effect:
  - Supports rule 6/long (guidance haircut), but with steeper numbers (C1).
  - Supports rule 12 (sell fast growers when growth slows).
  - Gives an exit threshold for rule 12 (2x market P/E or 3x PEG).

**F20. High ROE plus high growth beat the alternatives in every rolling 10-year window.** *Evidence with Hindsight (classified on the same 10 years' realised ROE and growth).*
- Companies with PAT ≥ INR 200 mn, rolling 10-year periods 1998-2018, average returns:
  - high ROE (above 15%) and high growth (above 15%): 27%;
  - low ROE, high growth: 21%;
  - high ROE, low growth: 14%;
  - low ROE, low growth: 6%;
  - Sensex: 14%.
- High-high beat the Sensex in all 11 windows. [MOSL-23, Exhibit 17]
- The longevity of ROE itself is rare. Of 136 companies with ROE above 13% in 1998 (PAT ≥ INR 200 mn), only 22 kept ROE above 13% every year to 2018. [MOSL-23, Exhibit 14]
- Rule effect:
  - Supports rule 6/long (ROE above 20% with growth).
  - Supports rule 7/long (longevity).
  - Needs a forward test (H15).

**F21. Consistency of profits beat volatility over 15 years.** *Evidence with Hindsight (Consistent/Volatile classified on the same window).*
- About 700 companies listed 2007-22 (Consistent defined as in F16):
  - 87% of Consistents outperformed the benchmark, against 33% of Volatiles;
  - average return 17% against 1% (Nifty 9%).
- Consistent companies in Volatile sectors did best, averaging 19% (e.g. Astral 45%, Page 36%, TTK 34%, Eicher 34%).
- In every rolling 10-year period 2000-22 and in every size band, Consistents beat the benchmark (alpha 6-12%) and Volatiles lagged it (−0.4% to −13%). [MOSL-27, Exhibits 10, 15, 17]
- Rule effect:
  - Supports rule 12/long (hold Consistents through quotational losses).
  - Refines rule 12: "Volatiles… must exit at an appropriate price, typically significantly higher than median P/B" (opinion drawn from the data).

**F22. Marcellus Consistent Compounders filter back-test.** *Evidence (manager's own marketing back-test); one regime (2000-2018).*
- Filter: double-digit year-on-year revenue growth and ROCE above the cost of capital, each year for 10 years in a row.
- Method: buy the passing stocks each year and hold each annual portfolio for 10 years without churn.
- Result for the windows 2000-10 to 2008-18: "returns of 20-30% p.a. and 8-12% outperformance relative to the Sensex". For holding periods over 3 years, volatility is said to be "similar to that of a Government Bond". [MARC-CCP Nov 2019]
- Mukherjea's stated mechanism (opinion): Indian dominant firms sustain ROCE well above a ~15% cost of equity for decades. Examples: Asian Paints, Page, HDFC Bank, Nestle. Median ROE was 31% in FY98-08 and 34% in FY09-18. [MARC-CCP Feb 2019]
- Rule effect:
  - Supports rule 6/long and rule 7/long.
  - Refines: the Marcellus test asks for 10 years of uninterrupted growth plus ROCE. That is stricter on consistency and looser on growth rate (10%+) than the 25% hurdle.

**F23. Survival and base rates: most small listed companies fail.** *Evidence.*
- Of 3,440 BSE-listed companies in Dec 2014, by Dec 2019:
  - 594 were no longer listed;
  - 209 had fallen 90-100%, 169 had fallen 80-90%, and 132 had fallen 70-80%.
- That makes 1,104 (32%) delisted or down 70%+ within 5 years. [MOSL-24, Exhibit 1]
- The size premium is absent on average:
  - "Small" (below the top-10% by market cap) minus big returned −58% cumulative over 1994-2013, mean −2.9% a year. [AJV-2014]
  - In the later series, SMB returned −1.09% a year with a 72.7% drawdown from Jan 1995, not recovered. [AJV-2017]
- Rule effect: refines rule 9. Small size is where long-term multibaggers start (F18), but the average small stock does not outperform and many disappear. Size is a condition that needs the quality, governance and survival gates (rules 1, 4), not a source of return on its own.

**F24. Indian value (HML) premium is positive, with a long drawdown in 2014-19.** *Evidence.*
- HML returned a mean 10.8% a year (log) over 1994-2013 and 15.3% (average annual) over 1994-2014. [AJV-2014]
- In the later series it is 5.99% a year, with a −56.9% drawdown from Jun 2014 to Nov 2019, not recovered. [AJV-2017]
- Rule effect: supports rule 8's low-valuation preference over the long run, with a regime flag (value lagged 2014-19, consistent with F17).

**F25. Quality (profitability and payout) earns significant alpha in India.** *Evidence.*
- The QMJ factor earns a four-factor alpha of 0.92% a month, and a long-only quality portfolio 0.69% a month.
- The alpha is "mainly driven by profitability and payout", linked to a tunnelling hypothesis (promoters diverting profits lowers both). Quality portfolios had "shorter drawdowns". [JPV-2022, abstract]
- Rule effect:
  - Supports rule 6/long (ROE above 20%).
  - Supports rule 3/governance: profitability and payouts as a governance signal.
  - Supports rule 1.4 (cash conversion).

**F26. Accounting quality: the dirtiest deciles underperform, and stay dirty.** *Evidence (manager research).*
- Marcellus's forensic screen (BSE 500 excluding insurers, 11 ratios, Jul 2006 to Sep 2022, semi-annual rebalance):
  - "adds around 5.5% per annum" versus an equal-weighted BSE 500;
  - returns fall and drawdowns rise from D1 to D10. [MARC-MQ]
- Persistence of forensic deciles from one year to the next: D1 stays D1 68% of the time, and D10 stays D10 66%. [MARC-deck25]
- Its 2017-24 return-by-decile chart is not monotonic: D8 returned 18.7% against D1's 15.9% ("Quality wins in the long run but the last 3 yrs were tough"). [MARC-deck25]
- Rule effect:
  - Supports rule 1.3 and 1.4 (governance; cash conversion as a gate) and rule 15's "never an integrity flag".
  - Regime flag: weaker in 2017-24.

**F27. Valuation matters less over very long horizons (MOSL's stated view).** *Opinion drawn from a 25-year study.*
- "Stock returns are slaves of earnings power and growth. In the very long run, valuations matter less." MOSL ignored valuation when shortlisting its 25-year portfolio.
- Of the top 500 companies in 1995, only 100 beat the benchmark over the next 25 years. [MOSL-25]
- Rule effect: supports rule 8/long ("can pay more for longevity"). But see F18: 100x entries were cheap. The tension is resolved by noting that MOSL's 25-year winners were mostly large (top 500) compounders, while 100x stocks started small and cheap.

**F28. Mayer (US, 1962-2014): 100-baggers took mostly 15-25 years and needed holding through deep falls.** *Evidence (US) and Opinion.*
- Median sales at the start of the run were about $170 m, so they "don't have to be tiny".
- The key trait is a high return on invested capital that can be reinvested.
- On owners: a "talented entrepreneur who owns a lot of stock, it puts the odds in your favor."
- Monster returned 100x in 10 years despite "a 40% decline, a 30% decline, and several 20%-plus declines". Investors fail because they "panic every time their stocks fall". [MAYER-2016]
- Regime/market flag: US data.
- Rule effect: supports rule 3 (owner-operators for the long term) and rule 12/long (exit only if the thesis breaks).

**F29. Phelps (US, 40 years to 1971): more than 365 stocks rose 100x; "A fast-growing company must be small."** *Opinion/US evidence, read only as quoted by MOSL.*
- On price: "A lemon that has been flattened by a steam roller has more juice in it than a piece of information the stock market has already discounted." [PHELPS via MOSL-19, MOSL-23]
- Rule effect: supports rule 9 and rule 10.

**F30. Kedia's SMILE.** *Opinion (practitioner heuristic, no data).*
- The letters, in his words: "S stands for Small in size… L stands for Large in aspiration… E stands for Extra-large market potential."
- As reported (not quoted): he wants management with 10-15 years in the same business, and uses the example of ₹500 cr growing to ₹5,000 cr over 5-10 years.
- His own words on management: "Jockey is more important than the horse."
- On valuation: "a theme does not mean stocks should be bought at any price." [KEDIA-2026]
- Rule effect: supports rule 10 as written. No evidence weight.

## 5. Cutting across horizons

**F31. Fall from the top: why companies dropped out of the top 100.** *Evidence.*
- 2005-15: 161 cases of Mega-to-Mid. Causes:
  - cyclical downturns 57 (35%);
  - management lapses 46 (29%);
  - reverse value migration 19 (12%);
  - quality trap 4 (2%);
  - plus capital misallocation and fads.
- 82 of the 112 companies were non-leaders.
- MOSL's lesson (opinion): "cyclical stocks… do not yield themselves to a Buy-and-Hold strategy… need to exit them before the cycle turns negative, even if it means selling a bit too soon." [MOSL-20, section 7]
- Excessive valuation case: Infosys in March 2000 at a P/E of 200x "took 7 years… to reach those price levels again". [MOSL-18]
- Rule effect:
  - Supports rule 12 (exit triggers) and rule 8 (cyclicals on mid-cycle).
  - Supports rule 3 (management) and the rule 12 trim at P/E above 50.

**F32. Debt and bruising.** *Evidence (case table).*
- Debt pile-up is "a common factor across many Bruised Blue Chips" (Jindal Steel, Suzlon, CG Power), and debt reduction "is a key healing factor". [MOSL-29, Exhibits 13, 21]
- Volatiles typically carry relatively high debt-equity. [MOSL-27, Exhibit 4]
- Rule effect: supports rule 4.

**F33. Parent and management quality drive emergence and endurance.** *Evidence (cited academic) and Opinion.*
- MOSL-18 cites McGahan & Porter (2003): for high performers, the "corporate-parent" (promoter) explains 18% of the emergence of abnormal profits and 19% of their sustainability, and segment-specific factors 43% and 34%. The year or economic cycle explains 2-3%.
- MOSL treats "a strong corporate-parent in a non-cyclical business" as raising the probability of emergence.
- Change of management or ownership recurs as a trigger: Britannia, CG Power (Murugappa), JB Chem (KKR), Persistent, ICICI Bank, IndusInd. [MOSL-18, MOSL-20, MOSL-28, MOSL-29]
- Rule effect:
  - Supports rule 1.2 (look at the promoter group and parent).
  - Supports rule 2 (management's record).
  - Refines: a change of control to a stronger parent is a positive event worth tagging. The rules currently treat promoter changes mainly as risks.

**F34. Value migration, niche leadership and profit pools are the dominant business themes.** *Evidence (classification of survivors) and Opinion.*
- 19 of the 47 100-baggers were value-migration beneficiaries, and almost all came from high-profit-pool sectors. [MOSL-19]
- Emergence amplifiers (F12) and Mid-to-Mega leadership (F8) point the same way.
- MOSL also warns (opinion): niche companies "seem to go through several rounds of trial and error… it may be prudent to buy into such companies only after they have secured their business model". [MOSL-19, §5.1.3]
- Rule effect: supports rule 7/long (large market) and rule 21 (sector tailwinds).

## 6. Stages of a multibagger and what was visible at the start

### 6.1 Stages
1. **Overlooked or depressed.**
   - Entry P/E was typically single-digit for 100-baggers (average about 6; 30 of 47 below 5x, F18).
   - It was at most 20 for emerging value creators (average 10, F12).
   - 70% of hockey-stick names were at 20x or less (F13). Mid-to-Megas had a median of 15x (F8).
   - Many began after a crash or a sector downturn (F6, F7).
2. **Earnings turn.**
   - Visible as ROE crossing 15% for the first time (F12), an EP quintile move up (F13), or growth already present: Mid-to-Mega screen used a prior 2-year PAT CAGR of at least 20% (F8).
   - MOSL-8 (opinion): "positive change in business conditions is a must… typically the starting point of the dismantling of pessimism."
3. **Re-rating.**
   - The P/E multiplies alongside earnings: about 4x over 12 years for 100-baggers (F18); 10 → 62 for Bajaj Finance and 5 → 38 for Ratnamani over 2013-22 (MOSL-28, Exhibit 2).
   - In the fastest 5-year runs, re-rating was about half the log return (F5).
   - Timing: none of the sources measures the lag from earnings turn to re-rating directly. This is an open item for the stage studies (H8).
4. **Compounding.**
   - High growth rarely lasts beyond 5-6 years (C1, F19).
   - Longer runs need a secular business plus a growth mindset: 80% of growth-advantage periods over 9 years were secular (F19).
5. **De-rating or exit.**
   - Triggers seen: cyclical downturns, management lapses, value migrating away, valuations at 2x market P/E or 3x PEG, and P/E in the 200x range (Infosys 2000) (F19, F31).

### 6.2 Drawdowns along the way
- Even blue chips that never got "bruised" fell an average 28% from the 5-year high to the 10-year low. Bruised ones fell 75% (F7).
- The 1998-2003 wealth creators' index fell 49.8% year on year in FY01, yet compounded at 35.5% over the 5 years (MOSL-8).
- The Indian market factor's worst drawdown was −74.6% (Sep 1994 to Sep 2001), taking 11 years to recover (AJV-2017).
- Mayer's Monster fell 40% and 30% during its 10-year 100x (F28, US).
- No source gave a systematic drawdown distribution for Indian multibaggers. This is a gap for H13.

### 6.3 What was observable at the start
| Signal | What the evidence shows | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Size | 100-baggers at about ₹250 cr average market cap; hockey-stick names mostly ranks 201-500; medium-term winners from ranks 101-300 | F18, F13, F8 |
| ROCE/ROE | ROE crossing 15% for the first time; Mid-to-Mega at 20%+; EP quintile 2-3 | F12, F8, F13 |
| Growth | Prior 2-year PAT CAGR ≥20% (Mid-to-Mega); most wealth from 25%+ growers; but 25%+ growth rarely persists past 5 years | F8, F14, C1 |
| Valuation | Low P/E at entry (6 for 100x, ≤20 typical); 50x+ entries lagged in 1998-2015; the post-2016 regime is looser | F18, C3, F17 |
| Promoter/parent | Strong parent or new management raises the odds | F33 |
| Balance sheet | Debt is common in bruised names; deleveraging helps healing | F32 |
| Accounting | Dirty forensic deciles underperform and persist | F26 |

## 8. Hypotheses for the back-test

Each is testable from annual statements, quarterly TTM EPS and sales, daily P/E, price and market-cap history.
- "Window" means a start date per quarter, 2006-2023, as in `plan.md`.
- "Excess return" means against the Nifty 500 TRI or the sample median.
- Report every result split by regime (2006-10, 2011-15, 2016-19, 2020-23) and by size band.

**Valuation**
- **H1. Entry P/E band (rule 8, the 50 cap).**
  - Test: 3-year and 5-year forward excess return and hit rate (winner by the horizon's definition) by TTM P/E band at entry: <10, 10-20, 20-30, 30-50, 50-75, >75. Universe: market cap ≥₹1,000 cr.
  - Expected from MOSL-23: excess falls sharply above 50 (−8% and −18% 3-year alpha in 1998-2015).
  - Key test: whether the 50+ penalty holds for 2016-23 starts (F17).
- **H2. P/E relative to market (rule 8, rule 12 trim).**
  - Test: forward 3-year excess return when stock P/E ÷ market (sample median) P/E is ≤1, 1-2, 2-3 or >3.
  - Expected: >2x underperforms (−5% and −11% 3-year alpha).
- **H3. Own-history anchor (rule 8).**
  - Test: forward 3-year excess return and win rate when TTM P/E is below or above the stock's own trailing 5-year and 10-year median P/E. Split Consistents (PAT fell >10% at most 3 times in the prior 10 years, never >50%, ending PAT ≥ starting PAT) from Volatiles.
  - Expected: below-median entries win more often for Consistents. For Volatiles, test P/B below its own 10-year median instead.
- **H4. PEG without foresight (rule 8).**
  - Test: forward 3-year excess return by PEG = TTM P/E ÷ trailing 3-year EPS CAGR, in bands 0-0.5, 0.5-1, 1-2, 2-3, >3, and negative.
  - Compare with the foresight PEG (realised next-3-year EPS CAGR) to measure how much of MOSL's PEG edge survives without hindsight.

**Growth**
- **H5. Growth persistence (rule 6/long haircut).**
  - Test: P(next 5-year PAT CAGR ≥25% | prior 5-year PAT CAGR ≥25%), and the same on TTM EPS, for companies with PAT ≥₹20 cr at the start. Repeat for 3-year spells.
  - Expected: about 10-30% (MOSL: 13/103 for 2003-08 → 2008-13).
  - Output: the base-case haircut per bucket.
- **H6. Emergence trigger (rule 2).**
  - Test: forward 5-year return and 10x hit rate for companies whose ROE (or ROCE) crosses 15% for the first time in 10+ years of history, with PAT ≥₹20 cr and P/E ≤20, against matched controls.
  - Expected: about 40% CAGR, +24% relative (MOSL 2001-08, n=17).
  - Amplifiers to test: top-3 revenue in the industry, high-profit-pool sector, consumer-facing.
- **H7. Mid-to-Mega screen (rules 6, 8, 9/medium).**
  - Screen: market-cap rank 101-300 (also 101-250), ROE ≥20%, prior 2-year PAT CAGR ≥20%, non-cyclical sector, P/E ≤25.
  - Test: P(rank ≤100 within 5 years), 5-year CAGR, and the 3x/5y hit rate against the unscreened rank 101-300 set.
  - Expected: crossover probability well above the 5-12% base rate.

**Size**
- **H8. Size and failure (rule 9).**
  - Test: by starting market cap (<₹500 cr, 500-3,000, 3,000-10,000, 10,000-25,000, >25,000 cr), report:
    - P(10x in 7-10 years);
    - P(3x in 5 years);
    - P(delisted or a ≥70% loss within 5 years).
  - Expected: highest multibagger rate and highest failure rate below ₹3,000 cr. MOSL-24: 32% of all listed companies delisted or lost ≥70% in 2014-19.

**Stages**
- **H9. Re-rating share and timing (rule 8's re-rating test).**
  - Test: decompose each winner's return into ln(EPS change) and ln(P/E change) per horizon.
  - Expected: median re-rating share about 0.5 for 5-year fastest (F5), about 0.3 for 10-year/100x (F18), and higher for 2-year winners.
  - Also measure the lag (quarters) from the first quarter of TTM EPS acceleration (QoQ TTM growth turning positive for 2+ quarters) to the start of the P/E expansion (P/E up 30%+ from its trailing low).
- **H10. Entry P/E of long winners (rule 8/long).**
  - Test: distribution of entry P/E for 10x-in-7-10-year winners against controls.
  - Expected: mostly ≤20 (MOSL: 70% of 25%+ 10-year CAGR names at ≤20; 100-baggers averaged 6).
  - Test whether any 10x winner started at P/E ≥50.

**Short-term signals**
- **H11. Post-earnings drift (rule 6/short).**
  - Test: for each results date, SUE proxy = (quarterly EPS − same quarter last year) ÷ price 6-12 days before. Measure decile excess return over days +2 to +64.
  - Expected: top − bottom ≈ +4-5% (HSY-2018).
  - Also test whether 2-year winners sat in the top SUE decile at the window start.
- **H12. Price momentum (rules 6, 11/short).**
  - Test: 12-1 month return quintile at the window start against the 2x-in-2-year hit rate and 6-12 month excess return.
  - Expected: top quintile has a higher hit rate (AJV: WML about 17-22% a year).
- **H13. Transitory against enduring (rules 6, 12/short).**
  - Test: among stocks that 3x in ≤2 years, the share giving back ≥50% within the next 3 years. Group by:
    - starting ROE ≥15% against <15%;
    - Consistent against Volatile PAT history;
    - re-rating share ≥0.7 against <0.7.
  - Expected: low-ROE, re-rating-led triplers give it back far more often (MOSL-8, 1998-2000).

**Drawdowns and exits**
- **H14. Drawdowns during winning runs (rule 12, rule 13).**
  - Test: for each winner, the maximum peak-to-trough drawdown during the run, and the number of ≥30% falls.
  - Then test how many would have been sold by the rule 12 triggers: 2 quarters of falling revenue and OPM; P/E >50; market cap >₹15-20k cr.
  - Expected: most long winners had at least one ≥40% drawdown.
- **H15. Exit thresholds (rule 12).**
  - Test: subsequent 1-year and 3-year excess return after a holding first crosses each of: TTM P/E >50; P/E >2x market P/E; trailing PEG >3; market cap rank entering the top 100.
  - Compare with holding on, to measure whether trimming adds value.

**Quality, balance sheet and recovery**
- **H16. High ROE plus high growth, forward version (rule 6/long).**
  - Test: classify on the prior 10 years (average ROE >15%, PAT CAGR >15%), then measure the next 5- and 10-year returns.
  - Expected: this forward version is weaker than MOSL-23's same-window 27%. Measure how much weaker.
- **H17. Economic-profit trajectory (rules 2, 6).**
  - Measure: EP = net worth × (ROE − 10%). Rank into quintiles at the start.
  - Test: P(25% CAGR over 10 years) by starting EP quintile, and by EP rising over the prior 3 years.
  - Expected: quintiles 2-3 with rising EP give about 18-21% hit rates (MOSL-28).
- **H18. Bruised quality (rules 2, 19).**
  - Screen: prior 10-year average ROE ≥20% (or top 50 by market cap), price ≥50% below the 5-year high, P/B <2.
  - Test: forward 3-year return and further downside, against quality stocks not down 50%.
  - Expected: higher forward return with low mortality (MOSL-29). Measure from the screen date, not the low.
- **H19. Leverage at the start (rule 4).**
  - Test: share of winners against controls with net debt/equity >0.8, interest cover <3x, or net debt/EBITDA >2.5x at the window start, and whether winners de-levered during the run.
- **H20. Cash conversion and accounting proxies (rule 1.4).**
  - Test: forward 3- and 5-year return and 10x hit rate by 5-year CFO/PAT (≥0.65, 0.4-0.65, <0.4) and by CFO/EBITDA quartile, from annual statements.
  - Expected: bottom bands lag and have higher failure rates (Marcellus, about 5.5% a year uplift from forensic screening).
