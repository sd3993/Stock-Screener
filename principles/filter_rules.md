# Filter rules: triage and the gate

Standards for the two filters before the deep analysis: triage (a script) and the gate (a judgement). Both apply `/principles/investing_rules.md`; this file holds only what the filters add.

## Both filters
- **Holdings are never filtered:** they skip triage's rejects and the gate.
- **Sector focus:** the gate takes the focus set first, and the reserve only when the focus set doesn't fill the positions (`/principles/sector_rules.md`, the sector focus); triage itself has no sector filter.
- **Competitive sectors:** in a sector with a surplus or a profit-capture verdict of partly or doesn't, a company needs its own evidence of beating competitors (share gains, orders at stable margins, a cost edge; growth above peers counts only with margins at or above the peers' median or rising toward it, since growth at the lowest margin is share bought on price; margins above the peers' median count even with slower growth when the filings show why, e.g. a cost, technology or mix edge, and volumes are holding); rising sector demand alone isn't enough. The analysis applies the same test.

## Triage (decided by `triage.py`; its `--help` has the exact rules)
- **Return math** at three projection lengths (2, 5 and 7 years; a stock's own visible-until date is only set in the analysis): the EPS growth needed for the CAGR hurdle at exit P/E = min(own 5-year median P/E, 30) (a script cannot judge re-rating evidence, so triage allows the re-rating; the gate and the analysis apply the rule), without dividends, against the growth visible in the trailing figures (conservative and optimistic).
- **Earnings evidence** (the earnings evidence rule): E1 acceleration, E2 re-rating candidate (a quality business at a P/E below its own range with growth intact), E3 sustained growth, E4 turnaround.
- **Queued for the gate:** a screen hit, or a non-screen name (the sweep) with earnings evidence, live return math and no hard flag; or an emerging company or participant (`/principles/sector_rules.md`) with no hard flag.
- **Rejected at triage only when clearly failed**, by a margin a data error is unlikely to flip: return math dead at every projection length even at the optimistic growth plus 5 points; promoter pledge above 25% of promoter holding; cash conversion below 0.3 over both 2 and 5 years; REITs, InvITs and investment companies. Anything near a line goes to the gate instead.

## The gate
- **A filter, not the analysis:** it removes only companies that clearly can't meet the rules; borderline ones go on to the analysis.
- **Guidance:** guidance from another company's document or older than 12 months counts as absent.
- **Return math** at the three projection lengths, at base-case EPS growth g: multiple ≈ (1+g)^years × exit P/E ÷ P/E now (no dividends), years = 2, 5 and 7. g = management's numeric guidance where given (profit growth implied by revenue and margin guidance), haircut where its record of delivery is poor, else the sustained 3-year record tempered by the latest two quarters; never the latest quarter's spike. Where the sector entry gives a dated tailwind that the company's own filings show it is capturing (orders, capacity), say so; it can support g only through the company's own figures and guidance, never by itself. Exit P/E, its logged exceptions and one-off normalisation: the valuation and return math rule; say which exception was used and, where the exit P/E is above the P/E now, the forward evidence for the re-rating.
- **Either period:** strong growth over the short term (the latest quarters) or the long term (3 years) is enough to look further; a company is not dropped for being strong in only one. The weaker period's dip or spike must be explained: its cause, and either a plan already resolving it (with filed evidence and a date) or the fact that it is resolved (the trajectory rule). An unexplained one is a REJECT reason only when it undermines the g used.
- **NEXT** when the best projection length's base-case CAGR (multiple^(1/years) − 1) is about 22% or more (rougher than the hurdle, so borderline cases reach S11) and no floor fails. Otherwise REJECT, or WATCH under the price watch or the growth watch below.
- **Price watch:** a company in the focus set that fails no floor and whose growth evidence holds, but whose best projection length's base-case CAGR falls short of the line only on price, is WATCH rather than REJECT. Its buy-below price is the price at which that CAGR reaches the 25% hurdle at the same g and exit P/E: buy-below = price now × ((1 + CAGR) / 1.25)^years. A company whose growth itself falls short is REJECT, unless the growth watch applies.
- **Growth watch:** a company in the focus set that fails no floor, has a clean or mixed promoter record, and falls short of the line on growth because of one named drag with a dated end in its filings (a loss-making new unit with a break-even date, capacity not yet commissioned, a plant shutdown, one-off contract losses) is WATCH rather than REJECT when, with the drag removed (its losses taken out, or its capacity on stream, with the cost of getting there counted), its best projection length's base-case CAGR reaches the line. It records the drag, the marker that ends it (what, by when) and the CAGR without the drag. Growth short with no named drag, or still short with the drag removed, is REJECT.
- **Emerging companies** (`/principles/sector_rules.md`): g may rest on the filed evidence of the new business (orders, approvals, dated capacity) where the trailing figures don't show it yet. With no numeric basis for g, the name goes NEXT when no floor fails, and the analysis decides. A **participant's** g rests on its established business, plus the emerging part only as far as filed evidence backs it.
- **Floors** (any one fails → REJECT): cash conversion below the non-negotiables' floor of 0.4 over 3 years, or CFO negative in both latest half-years (where it fails only on an estimate, e.g. acceptances the company splits only at March, it is a flag, not a floor: it rejects only together with other bad or likely-bad evidence, and otherwise the analysis weighs it; a ratio in the 0.4-0.65 band that meets neither of its paths is not a gate reject: the analysis judges it as Fail or Bent); net debt/equity above the cash and debt rule's line (lenders: that rule's lender note instead); pledge per the pledge rule; promoter selling that fails the promoter selling and buying rule's tests; integrity flags (the integrity flags rule); holding/investment companies; a one-quarter profit spike off a tiny base with no guidance.
- **Re-gating:** a company already gated this quarter isn't re-gated unless it filed something new, its price has fallen 20% or more since its gate check, or a rule change could change its decision; a price watch is re-gated when its price is at or below its buy-below price, or its earnings have risen enough to lift the buy-below price to the price; a growth watch is re-gated when its marker is met or its date passes, or when new results are filed (to see whether the drag is shrinking).
