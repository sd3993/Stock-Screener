# Investment rules

Rules are cited by their heading name. Who may change what:

- **Rules file:** nothing here changes without the user's OK. S11 subagents propose sector-note changes (old and new wording, reason, evidence); the orchestrator passes each proposal to the user and applies only what the user accepts. No changelog. A change re-runs S11 only for companies in that sector whose verdict could flip; a company's main analysis and its rechecks use the same version of the file.
- **Expert advice:** for any step, you may search the internet for advice from proven, reputed, experienced investors and analysts and propose it for this file, tagged with its source.

- **General rules** are Shouvik's; several act as gates. **Sector notes** sit as sub-points under the general rule they adjust, and exist only for sectors already researched in depth (list at the end); a company in a sector with no notes is analysed on the general rules and flagged.
- **Tags** say where each point came from: **[Yours]** Shouvik's rule; **[Expert: name, work]** a named, proven practitioner; untagged sector notes were found in companies' filings during research. Opinion pieces and anonymous websites are never sources.

## Holding period and the hurdle [Yours]
- **Holding period:** how long a stock is held depends on its evidence-backed revenue growth and visibility. Each stock has one **visible-until date**: the date up to which its own evidence backs its growth (the visibility rule), reset every quarter. It is an estimate that moves, never a sell date. The return math is projected to that date, never less than 2 years or more than 7 ahead.
- **Discovery stage:** undiscovered / early / recognised / crowded, from the sector's valuation against its own history, price strength, institutional-holding change and recent issuance, or the company's own where it differs (with a reason). Stage limits how much re-rating the case may assume (none when crowded) and sets how a position is sized (the position size and count rule).
- **Hurdle (an entry test):** base-case expected return of at least **25% a year (CAGR)** to the visible-until date, and the bear case no worse than ~0.8x. A candidate whose thesis passes but whose price fails the hurdle or another price-based rule (PEG) goes on the watchlist with a buy-below price, so short-lived price moves don't make it vanish. A holding is not sold on it: while its thesis is intact it is kept below the hurdle, flagged in red with every reason shown, and gets no adds until it clears again.
- **Holdings are always listed:** every stock held, even one with an exit call, stays on the list, flagged the same way for every reason (non-negotiable fail, thesis broken, below hurdle), so a temporary or marginal miss can be judged before selling.
- **Ranking:** by base-case expected CAGR (highest return in the least time), with evidence quality and the visible-until date shown alongside.
- **Watchlist:** stocks waiting on something before a buy or a top-up: a candidate above its buy-below price, a thesis waiting on a dated piece of evidence, a holding waiting for its top-up trigger. Each entry says what is awaited (a price or a dated marker), the evidence so far and the next check date. Only a new deep analysis (S11) takes a stock off the list or the watchlist.
- "100x" is a tag on stocks whose visibility runs 3 years or more.

## Non-negotiables (gates)
Judged on today's level AND the direction over the last 2-3 quarters (other factors use 1-2 quarters). One can pass while still below its line only if it is not too low today, has improved for 2+ consecutive quarters, and the cause is found and logged. [Yours] Permanent loss comes from fraud, leverage and forced selling. [Expert: Howard Marks, *The Most Important Thing*]

- **Pledge** (incl. pledges of the promoter's holding company): passes at 0; or at ≤10% of promoter holding while being released over the last 2-3 quarters with no new pledge. Fails above ~20%, or on any new pledge in the last 3 quarters. [Yours]
- **Promoter selling and buying** (promoter group as a whole) [Yours]: a sale is never a fail by itself; a promoter with a large stake is expected to sell some so institutions and the public can build positions. Look for the reason (exchange filings, call transcripts, AGM proceedings, filed press releases) and log it with the sale's size, but judge on these tests:
   - **Selling fails** when the promoter group ends below 50% after the sales, or when results worsen for 2-3 consecutive quarters during or around the sales (a one-off dip doesn't count). "Results" means profit and operating cash flow together: profit growth with weak cash conversion around the sales counts as worsening.
   - **Selling also fails** when it comes shortly before an adverse event the company later discloses (a lost order or customer, regulatory action, a downgrade), or when it funds a stressed group company (seen as group pledges or group debt).
   - **Selling passes** otherwise, including sales followed by good results.
   - **Buying passes** at a promoter-group holding of 40% or more, whatever the recent results, when it is real cash buying (open market, or a preferential issue or warrants paid in cash, not ESOPs) and there is no pledge; any weakness is left to the full analysis (S11).
   - A co-promoter family's or PE promoter's exit is judged on the same tests and logged.
   - All sectors: weigh a small promoter-group sale against promoter buying or preferential subscription in the prior 12 months (the net flow), not on its own.
   - All sectors: sales by promoter-group family members count, even when the CMD is buying; check the promoter-group pages of the SHP each quarter.
   - All sectors: check the parent promoter's own pledge, debt and credit rating; a highly pledged or downgraded parent fails the child. [Yours]
   - Foreign-parent promoters (e.g. a rating agency owned by a global parent): judged on the parent's stated intent and stake trend, not the holding line alone.
- **Integrity flags:** integrity flags are absolute (auditor resigning over concerns, money moved to related parties, forensic audit, SEBI/ED/fraud orders). Softer items (related-party deals, a resolved regulatory notice, board changes) are judged on trend. [Yours] Related-party transactions and auditor changes are the first forensic checks. [Expert: Saurabh Mukherjea, *Coffee Can Investing* (forensic accounting chapter)]
   - All sectors: an auditor resignation, or a third preferential issue inside ~14 months, is checked before any return math.
- **Cash conversion** (operating cash flow / profit): passes at ≥0.65 over 3-5 years; or at ≥0.4 over 3 years when each of the last two half-years is better (cash flow is published only half-yearly, so quarterly debtor/inventory days and collections support the trend). Fails below 0.4 even if improving. [Yours] Cash flow, not reported profit, is the test of earnings quality. [Expert: Mukherjea, *Coffee Can Investing*; Buffett, owner earnings, Berkshire letter 1986]
   - **Capital goods / EPC / process equipment:** milestone billing makes Q4 dispatches land as March receivables; judge over 3 years and the H1/H2 pattern, never one year. A promise of "realisation next quarter" is checked against the next half-year cash flow.
   - **Defence electronics:** long development cycles and PSU customers make CFO/PAT structurally low. Treat as structural unless the cash conversion cycle has fallen for 2+ half-years.
   - **Metal recycling / import-scrap models:** heavy hedged and in-transit inventory gives weak H1 and strong H2 every year; judge on full years and 3-year totals.
   - **Packaging / polymer converters:** stocking raw material ahead of polymer price rises and new-capacity WIP depress CFO; check whether the inventory days return after the price move.
   - **Companies with large treasury books (e.g. rating agencies, cash-rich software):** interest and fair-value gains sit in profit but in investing cash flow (Ind AS 7), so CFO/PAT reads low. Compute CFO / (PAT − post-tax other income) as well; pass on that figure when the gap is fully explained by treasury income.

## Trajectory, plans and root cause (every rule)
Judge each metric on three things together: the trailing value, its direction (last 1-2 quarters for ordinary factors; 2-3 quarters for non-negotiables), and any plan already in execution with a milestone within 1-2 quarters (capacity commissioning, price increases, working-capital release, order execution, debt repayment). For every negative, log: (a) the cause, with its source; (b) temporary or structural; (c) how management handled the same problem before (past cycles, delivered vs missed guidance); (d) whether it is improving now. A weak trailing number with a found, temporary cause and an improving trend is not a rejection; a good trailing number with a deteriorating latest quarter is a warning. Outside the integrity flags, weigh what a stock passes against what it fails. [Yours] Know why the numbers moved. [Expert: Peter Lynch, *One Up on Wall Street*] Cheap stocks with improving fundamentals beat cheap stocks without. [Expert: Joseph Piotroski, "Value Investing" (2000)] Earnings acceleration. [Expert: William O'Neil, *How to Make Money in Stocks*]
- **Likelihood of scenarios** [Yours]: the base case follows the most likely path; if it needs several uncertain things to all go right, mark evidence quality low and use the likelier path. The bear case is a realistic setback (one or two things going wrong), not everything bad at once. Any case, good or bad, that needs many independent things to coincide is taken with a grain of salt, not as fact. A thesis that breaks is rewritten if the stock still passes as a new candidate on the new one (the exit rules); otherwise the verdict changes.
- **Turnaround checks** [Yours]: 2-3 quarters of sequential (QoQ) improvement, not YoY base effects; cause classified structural vs cyclical/one-off and confirmed by 2+ independent sources; operating cash flow follows profit within 2-4 quarters; promoter buying or holding at the low; stock still trough-priced; balance sheet can survive until the tailwind arrives; tailwind specific and dated; a reversal in the next 1-2 quarters is set as a marker (a re-analysis, never an exit by itself). Even when recent results are declining, check forward guidance, order book and upcoming strategic changes. Multibaggers are best bought at their low point with strong, dated tailwinds.
- **All sectors, guidance record:** keep a per-company guidance-vs-actual scorecard [Yours]; score delivered vs guided for the last 4-8 quarters before using guidance. A cut mid-year or a missed own floor means the next guidance gets a haircut in the base case. Judge management's candour about bad news. [Expert: Philip Fisher, *Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits*, point 14]
- **Capital goods / EPC:** a quarter's collapse can come from deliberately delayed order intake; fixed-price contracts then lock the margin reset for the year, so the "temporary" call must say for how long.
- **Programme-driven businesses (Kavach, single large government programmes):** when management itself says programme revenue may decline after a date, earnings are at a programme peak; base case must not extrapolate the peak.

## Use of history [Yours]
History may restrain a forecast, never raise one. An opinion or forecast never rests on what happened before (a past cycle, how similar companies or stocks did, a pattern across the market, a back-test) unless the repeat is mechanical: a solid, named factor or trigger that is in place again and causes it. Only then is the past looked up. Three uses of the past stand:
- **Mechanical repeats:** how a business's accounts and contracts behave (billing terms, accounting treatment, a regulated reset). The sector notes are of this kind.
- **The same actor's record:** a management's delivery against its own guidance, a promoter's past conduct.
- **The no-change default:** a forecast starts from the company's own current figure, and any change from it needs forward evidence.

## Governance (other)
Promoter holding above 50% (40% for a promoter group that is buying, per the promoter selling and buying rule), judged by trend: a fall of more than 5 points in 3 years passes only if it meets the promoter selling and buying rule's tests (or is dilution, below). Main board preferred; SME allowed only with high conviction, sized so one lot keeps the stock within 6-7% of the portfolio and its sector within the sector cap (the allocation and rebalancing rule). Beyond 5 years of visibility: owner-operators preferred. [Yours; Expert: Christopher Mayer, *100 Baggers*]
- **All sectors:** a stake fall from equity issues or warrant conversions is dilution, not selling, but counts against "no repeated dilution" (the cash and debt rule).
- **EPC / group-captive businesses:** revenue concentrated in a sister company is a related-party exposure: discount the exit P/E and check pricing and receivables from the group.

### Customers and markets [Yours]
Avoid companies heavily reliant on a very small list of customers. Prefer high exports, especially to developed markets (US, EU, UK, UAE, Singapore and similar).

## Cash and debt
Debt is fine when it funds capex backed by orders or contracts and the balance sheet can carry it: net debt/equity below 0.8, current ratio ≥1.25, interest coverage (EBIT / interest) ≥3x, net debt / EBITDA ≤2.5x. Debt that funds receivables or inventory (customers not paying) is a cash-conversion problem, not a capex one. No repeated equity dilution to fund growth. [Yours; Expert: Piotroski (falling leverage, rising current ratio, no new shares); Lynch]
- **All sectors, what counts as debt:** compute net debt both with and without lease liabilities (Ind AS 116) and report both; leases are debt-like obligations. [Expert: Aswath Damodaran, *Damodaran on Valuation* (operating leases as debt)] Vendor financing, usance letters of credit and acceptances for capex count as debt. Margin money and earmarked issue proceeds are not free cash.
- **Lenders (banks, NBFCs, brokers, insurers):** borrowing and deposits are the business, so the net debt/equity, current ratio and net debt/EBITDA tests don't apply. Judge capital adequacy, asset quality (gross and net NPA, credit cost) and leverage against the regulator's limits instead.
- **IT system integrators with leased equipment:** lease liabilities can grow fast and must be in the debt tests.
- **Equipment rental (cranes):** fleet capex is debt-funded by design; track interest cover trend and management's own D/E ceilings. A ceiling raised every year is guidance creep and a negative.
- **Spread businesses with fast growth (speciality carbon, chemicals):** borrowings that jump with receivables and shrinking supplier credit inside a half-year are working-capital debt, not capex debt.

## Government customers
Judged by outcomes: debtor days above ~120 or cash conversion below 0.65 fails. Government-dependent names are capped (the allocation and rebalancing rule). RBI, NPCI, Railways and defence buyers count as government; PSU customers often pay late. [Yours]
- **Defence electronics:** debtor days of 280-300 on government/PSU customers are normal for the model, so the 120-day line fails such names unless collections have improved for 2+ half-years.
- **Government IT projects:** go-live/milestone billing pushes debtor days up as order sizes grow; check >1-year receivables and ECL. Long OEM payables can offset (net cycle matters).
- **Healthcare paid by government schemes:** read the receivables ageing note: the share over 1 year and the ECL/write-offs, not just debtor days (which can fall while ageing worsens).
- **Water / Jal Jeevan Mission EPC:** state-scheme execution slowdowns hit both revenue and receivables.

## Earnings evidence
- **Visibility of about 2 years:** acceleration: latest quarter EPS +25% or more with sales +20% or more, rising over 2-3 quarters (O'Neil). Plus a dated catalyst within 2-4 quarters. Do not chase post-results spikes.
- **Visibility of 3-5 years:** EPS growth strong enough to clear the hurdle at a justified exit P/E, or lower growth plus a re-rating from a low P/E that rests on forward evidence (the valuation and return math rule), or a verified turnaround.
- **Visibility beyond 5 years:** sustained high EPS growth with ROCE above 20% and a long reinvestment runway; guidance counts only as far as the company's own delivery record supports it (the guidance record note).

[Yours; Expert: O'Neil (CAN SLIM); Lynch]
- **All sectors, one-offs:** strip deferred-tax reversals, MAT credits, tariff refunds, fire losses, write-offs and exceptional items before growth or P/E.
- **Software products:** licence-deal timing makes single years lumpy; a shift from licences to subscriptions lowers near-term revenue without a business decline. Separate the two before calling a slowdown structural.
- **CPaaS / messaging:** SMS pricing erosion is structural; growth must come from WhatsApp/RCS mix.
- **Commodity-linked specialities (gelatin, masterbatches):** volume growth with falling realisations is not earnings growth; check realisation per unit.

## Visibility (sets the visible-until date, decided in S11)
The visible-until date is the furthest date to which the company's own evidence backs its revenue growth, set from that evidence (the order book's executable period, the commissioning date and ramp of funded capacity, the last year its guidance covers) within the band whose test holds. A dated sector tailwind supports it only through the company's own orders, capacity and guidance, never by itself. [Yours]
- **About 2 years:** already contracted or in the numbers: executable orders at least equal to next year's revenue, capacity already running, price increases passed through.
- **3-5 years:** order book 1.5-2.5x revenue, or funded capacity commissioning within 1-2 years.
- **Beyond 5 years:** market at least 10x current revenue; growth that can last 10-15 years; funded plans for 3-4x capacity.

[Yours; Expert: Agrawal, QGLP (Longevity)]
- **Capital goods / EPC / defence:** count only firm orders filed with the exchange; L1 positions, LoIs and MoUs don't count (the news and events rule). Check the order book's executable period, not just its size. [Yours, news and events rule]
- **Export-heavy engineering:** order books can be stuck by tariffs or shipping disruption; tag export orders by destination risk.

## Moats [Yours]
A business that is hard to copy keeps high returns for longer, and that length drives compounding more than any one year's growth. Every deep analysis (S11) judges the moat:
- **Kinds:** approvals and qualification cycles of years (defence, nuclear, aerospace, pharma), regulatory licences, technology or patents, being designed into the customer's product (costly to switch), scarce assets or the lowest cost, process know-how.
- **Evidence, not claims:** returns on capital above its cost through the latest down year; costs passed on to customers; few competitors today (the hand-picked peers); repeat orders from the same customers; how long a new entrant's qualification takes. A company calling itself unique counts for nothing.
- **Tests:** a barrier without pricing power (a sole supplier whose government buyer sets the price) is narrow at most; a niche must still leave room to grow to the visible-until date; name what could breach it (a technology change, cheap imports, a large company entering).
- **Verdict:** none, narrow or wide, with the evidence and the threats; shown beside the base-case CAGR.
- **How it counts:** only through the analysis inputs, never as a bonus past the hurdle or any other test. A wide moat with evidence can support a later visible-until date (the visibility rule), a milder bear case, an exit P/E nearer the peers' median within the cap, and sizing nearer target (position size follows conviction).

[Yours; Expert: Pat Dorsey, *The Little Book That Builds Wealth*; Michael Mauboussin, *Measuring the Moat*; Agrawal, QGLP (Longevity)]

## Valuation and return math
Measured on forward earnings: base and bear EPS paths to the stock's visible-until date (the holding period rule), built from guidance, order book and capacity coming online (S11, price-independent). Expected multiple = EPS at that date × exit P/E (+ dividends) ÷ today's price; expected CAGR = multiple^(1/years) − 1 (computed live in the sheet from the S11 figures). The exit P/E is where the P/E is expected to settle at that date, never a sell trigger. The exit P/E is no higher than today's P/E unless forward evidence supports a re-rating: growth recovering or accelerating, shown in the company's orders, capacity or guidance, or an announced buyback (a P/E below peers supports it; a P/E below the stock's own history is no reason by itself). The ceiling either way is the stock's own 5-year P/E ("Historical PE 5Years" in screener.in's screens, the 5-year median), capped at 30 (40 only under the exception below); where that is below today's P/E, the math assumes the de-rating. Logged exceptions: recent listings use the median of listed peers; when the own figure was inflated by years of depressed or loss-making profit (turnarounds, post-IPO froth, boom years) a lower peer-based or judged exit P/E is used. Cyclicals (metals, commodity chemicals, capital goods, logistics, cranes): mid-cycle earnings cap the earnings used when trailing profit is above them (at the peak the P/E looks low); from below mid-cycle a recovery is assumed only on forward indicators (the buying after a fall rule). Profit is first normalised for one-offs (the earnings evidence rule's one-offs note). For high-payout stocks, dividends received count toward the target. Every listing shows the share of the return that comes from re-rating, and a bear case. PEG between 0 and 1 preferred; a negative PEG (falling or negative earnings) or a PEG above 2 is avoided.
- **Buy-below price:** when the hurdle only works at a P/E above the stock's own average, set a buy-below price instead of buying now (the watchlist). [Yours]
- **Exit P/E above 30:** up to 40, as a logged exception, only when all three hold: the math is at its 7-year ceiling and the evidence backs growth of 20% a year or more beyond it, so the growth-at-exit check is met; the stock's own 5-year median P/E and its hand-picked peers' medians are at or above the figure used; the bear case is still computed at 30 or below. [Yours]

[Yours; Expert: Lynch (PEG; cyclicals on mid-cycle earnings); Benjamin Graham & David Dodd, *Security Analysis* (average earnings over a cycle); Agrawal/MOSL Wealth Creation Studies (reasonable price non-negotiable; PEG; re-rating on recovering RoE)]

**Exit P/E sanity checks (S11)** [Yours]:
- **Hand-picked peers:** name 3-6 genuinely comparable listed companies (from the annual report's competitor mentions and the business mix, not the data provider's sector label) and compare the exit P/E with their 5-year median P/Es and the stock's own history; explain any gap.
- **Growth at exit:** the exit P/E should be no more than about 2x the EPS growth rate (%) expected at the visible-until date (an exit PEG of ~2 at most); e.g. growth slowing to 12% supports an exit P/E of about 24 at most.
- Both sit under the cap (30, or 40 under the exception).

**Re-rating share** = ln(exit P/E ÷ today's P/E) ÷ ln(expected multiple): the part of the return that comes from the P/E change rather than earnings growth (negative when the P/E is expected to fall).
- **All sectors, SME-era histories:** a 5-year median that includes the NSE Emerge / BSE SME years is treated as inflated; the cap binds and a peer-based check is logged.
- **All sectors, boom-year re-ratings:** when 1-2 boom years lift the 5-year median well above the stock's longer history, use a judged exit between the longer-run median and the cap.
- **Capital goods / EPC / cranes:** check whether trailing earnings are at, below or above mid-cycle (industry down year, margin vs 5-year average) and say which; a turnaround off near-zero profit inflates the median, so an exit of ~25 is logged.
- **Spread businesses near record EBITDA (coal-tar pitch, carbon black, gelatin):** 30 is a ceiling, not a target; test sensitivity at 25.
- **Programme-peak earnings** (the trajectory rule's programme note): exit P/E about 25.
- **Market premium for optionality** (aerospace, semiconductors, LNG): a P/E far above the cap with growth under ~25% is a de-rating risk, not a reason to raise the exit P/E.

## Exit rules
- **Thesis markers** [Yours]: every position has 4-6 dated markers set at entry, checked each quarter: at least two on revenue growth and visibility (order book, capacity, guidance delivered), judged over the trailing four quarters, never a single one; a sector-level one where the company's sector has a map entry. A broken marker triggers a full re-analysis at once; otherwise the full re-analysis is half-yearly.
- **One exit test** [Yours]: a position ends only when a re-analysis finds its thesis broken, or on an integrity flag (the integrity flags rule: exit review at once, whatever the markers say). A thesis is broken when the evidence no longer backs the growth and visibility it rests on and the cause is structural, when its visibility has run out with nothing new to extend it, or when a non-negotiable fails on its own test. The stock is then judged as a new candidate at today's price: it stays, on a rewritten thesis, if it passes; otherwise it gets an exit call.
- **Never exit reasons by themselves** [Yours]: a weak quarter, or a run of them with a found, temporary cause; a price fall or spike; a high valuation; a market cap above the size preference. Each goes through the quarterly marker check and nothing more.
- **Trim** [Yours]: only when a position's weight crosses its trim line (the position size and count rule), back to that line, or when its sector crosses the sector trim line (the allocation and rebalancing rule).
- **Exit calls** [Yours]: every exit call says whether it is urgent, naming the dated event behind it (a results date, a pledge invocation, a lock-in expiry, a regulatory order), or not urgent; the user picks the time.
- Never add to a damaged thesis. Re-verify data each quarter. Buying back a stock sold earlier is fine if it qualifies again.

[Yours; Expert: Lynch; Mayer]

## Position size and count
- **Target** [Yours]: about 7% of the portfolio a stock; higher only when the analysis and evidence strongly justify it, with the reason logged. The count follows from how many pass, never from filling slots.
- **Below target** [Yours]: no fixed starter size. A position below its target is sized to its situation: what it is waiting for (a marker, a price, filed evidence), conviction, how thinly it trades (emerging companies, 100x tags, undiscovered-stage names and micro caps are harder to exit) and, for SME stocks, the minimum lot. It is added to as what it waits for arrives.
- **Trim line** [Yours]: 15% of the portfolio; higher only with a logged higher target.
- **By stage** [Yours]: a crowded stock with visibility of about 2 years is avoided.

[Yours; Expert: Agrawal (15-20 stocks, 10% single-stock cap at MOSL MF)]

## Bent-rule tier (taking risk with support)
A stock may bend up to 2-3 rules (never an integrity flag; non-negotiables only within the floors in the non-negotiables) when the other principles give strong support: (a) asymmetric payoff: the hurdle's base-case and bear-case tests both hold; (b) the bent metric has improved for 2-3 quarters and/or the Piotroski score is 6 or more; (c) valuation at or below its own history; (d) management has fixed similar problems before. Size: below target, per the position size and count rule; add only once the bent metric is back inside the rule. All bent-rule names together are capped (the allocation and rebalancing rule). Each has a written kill condition and a review date. Breaching the stock or sector cap needs confirmed visibility (filings, contracts, notified policy). [Yours; Expert: Marks; Mohnish Pabrai, *The Dhandho Investor*; Piotroski; Kedia]

## News and events
Counts only when confirmed in writing by the official source: exchange filings for companies; PIB, gazette, ministry or regulator for government. Firm orders and notified policies only, not MoUs, LoIs or L1 positions. Size the event against revenue; note when it reaches the P&L. Be sceptical of good news near promoter selling, a preferential issue or a QIP. Check whether the price already moved. Guidance and news come only from exchange disclosures (transcripts, presentations, results releases, AGM proceedings, annual reports, announcements), never from news sites. [Yours]

## Allocation and rebalancing [Yours]
- **Position size follows the stock's total potential and conviction**, and how well the thesis is verified. A rise in them drives buys and adds; trims and exits follow the exit rules only.
- **Caps**, as shares of the portfolio: one stock at its target and trim line (the position size and count rule); one sector: at most 2 stocks, so each has room to grow, and a trim line at ~25% (no buy or add takes a sector past it; above it, the sector's holding with the weakest thesis or lowest base-case CAGR is trimmed until the sector is back at the line); higher only when the analysis and evidence strongly justify it, with the reason logged. A sector already holding more than 2 stocks gets no new buy or add, and its extra holdings are flagged for the user, never exited for that alone (the exit rules); government-dependent names 20-25%; all bent-rule names together ≤30%; all emerging companies together 15-20% (participants, established companies with an emerging business, are not counted: `/principles/sector_rules.md`).
- **Swap candidates** [Yours]: each holding is compared with the List; where a List name's base-case CAGR beats the holding's by 5 points or more, it is shown beside that holding as a swap candidate, with both return paths. Never an exit call: the holding keeps its status and the user decides.
- Add when a stock falls with the market and its thesis is intact (where visibility is about 2 years: only if the catalyst is still ahead). Don't leave much capital idle, but never fill weight with weak names: unfilled weight sits in a liquid fund.
- **Execution** [Yours]: Wave 1 and Wave 2 targets are a percentage of the final (Wave 2) position target. Front-load Wave 1 at 70% on confirmed low-YoY-base beats; execution-dependent low-base stocks 50%; high-YoY-base stocks expected to print negative headline YoY profit 0-20% Wave 1 / 80-100% Wave 2, to buy the post-results dip. Don't sell core holdings that already sit above their Wave 1 cap. No intraday trades (₹30 + GST brokerage, delivery STT/DP charges, FIFO resets the LTCG clock).

## Buying after a fall
A falling price is not a negative on its own: when the business is intact and the fall has a found, temporary cause, the risk is lower, not higher. Buy in 2-3 tranches. Prefer names where the hurt metric has already turned for at least one quarter over catching a falling price (avoids value traps). For cyclicals, buy when earnings are depressed and the cycle indicators turn, not when the trailing P/E looks low. [Yours; Expert: Marks; Piotroski; Lynch; Rakesh Jhunjhunwala (crashes as the time to accumulate quality)]
